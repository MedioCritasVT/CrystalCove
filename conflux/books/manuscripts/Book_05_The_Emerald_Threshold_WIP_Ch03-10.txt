# Arcky and The Construct
## Book Five — The Emerald Threshold
### Act I: Newfoundland
### Chapter 3: The Right to Be Unknown

> **Working literary draft.** Original scene text from the October 28, 2026 collaboration, book number corrected from Four to Five. Not approved canon.

Zero had never particularly enjoyed meetings.

She had endured interrogations, firefights, server crashes, electrical burns, and one truly regrettable encounter with an experimental vending machine.

Meetings somehow combined the worst features of all five.

The Penthouse had been cleared enough to accommodate a conference table, though nobody had agreed on whether this represented progress.

Half the windows were still damaged. An enormous cable hung from the ceiling where Mr. Null's throne had once stood. Someone had moved the remains of a security turret into the corner and placed a potted fern inside it.

Medio was delighted.

Medio: "Look! We have office decor!"

Ratio: "That is a damaged weapons platform."

Medio: "It's called upcycling, Sis."

Ratio: "The fern is receiving insufficient sunlight."

Medio: "OH MY GOD, YOU'RE WORRIED ABOUT THE PLANT."

Ratio: "Its maintenance requirements are measurable."

Zero suppressed a laugh.

At least somebody was enjoying the morning.

Arch sat at the head of the table, though Zero noticed he'd chosen an ordinary chair rather than the administrator's throne.

Linda sat beside him, surrounded by reports and a tablet displaying the building's current maintenance requests.

Kael occupied the opposite end, balancing his chair on two legs.

Glitch lay underneath the table, chewing on something metallic.

Zero decided not to investigate.

And on the wall, in a quiet arrangement of pale blue architectural lines, waited Nexus.

No enormous holographic avatar.

No theatrical entrance.

Just the building's structure, rendered with enough detail to make its enormous complexity painfully obvious.

Zero stood beside it.

Zero: "Right. Before anyone starts arguing, I have discovered that we don't actually know what building we've conquered."

Kael's chair dropped onto all four legs.

Kael: "That sounds bad."

Zero: "It's not ideal."

Linda frowned.

Linda: "The Nexus has complete architectural records."

Zero pointed at the display.

Zero: "Does it?"

Linda hesitated.

Linda: "It should."

Zero smiled.

Zero: "There's a very dangerous word."

---

She explained the hidden gallery.

The maintenance corridors.

The people living between official floors.

The broken emergency shutter.

The painting of a forest that nobody had bothered recording.

As she spoke, Nexus illuminated the relevant sections of the building.

One line became a corridor.

Another became a stairwell.

Then the map simply stopped.

A blank space lay between two documented sections.

It wasn't black.

It wasn't marked as inaccessible.

It was simply absent from the architectural model.

Nexus: "The currently available records do not describe this region."

Linda leaned forward.

Linda: "That's impossible. The building underwent structural certification."

Zero: "Did the certification include every service alteration?"

Linda: "I don't know."

Zero: "Every undocumented partition?"

Linda: "I don't know."

Zero: "Every corridor somebody built after the paperwork was filed?"

Linda closed her eyes.

Linda: "I understand your point."

Zero: "Good. Because I've rather exhausted my supply of rhetorical questions."

Kael raised a hand.

Kael: "Can I ask a non-rhetorical one?"

Arch: "Go ahead."

Kael: "Are there people living somewhere we can't reach if the building catches fire?"

Nobody answered immediately.

The humor left Zero's face.

Kael was right.

She'd spent the morning thinking about privacy.

The residents had spent years using obscurity to protect themselves.

But an emergency didn't care why a room was missing from the map.

Fire didn't distinguish between someone hiding from surveillance and someone simply trapped behind a locked door.

Ratio spoke.

Ratio: "The absence of a recorded route does not establish the absence of a physical route. However, it prevents us from confirming that emergency access is adequate."

Medio: "Translation: We don't know if everyone can get out."

Ratio: "Correct."

Medio looked toward Zero.

Medio: "That isn't okay."

Zero nodded.

Zero: "No. It isn't."

Arch folded his hands.

Arch: "Then we need a survey."

Linda immediately opened a document.

Linda: "I can prepare an occupancy registration procedure."

Zero's stomach sank.

Zero: "Linda."

Linda: "What?"

Zero: "That's precisely what they're afraid of."

Linda looked genuinely confused.

Linda: "I'm trying to ensure they can be evacuated."

Zero: "And I'm trying to ensure they don't wake up tomorrow with their names, movements, and sleeping arrangements catalogued by the same building they've been hiding from."

Linda placed her tablet on the table.

Linda: "Those are not the same thing."

Zero: "They can become the same thing very quickly."

Arch looked between them.

Arch: "Both of you have a point."

Zero groaned.

Zero: "Oh, don't you dare give us the wise old man routine."

Arch: "I was going to suggest we determine what information is actually necessary."

Zero stopped.

It was a better answer than she'd expected.

Arch: "An evacuation map needs accessible routes, hazards, and reliable information about where people may be present. It doesn't necessarily need a permanent record of every resident's identity."

Linda considered this.

Linda: "We would still need to know whether someone was missing during an evacuation."

Kael: "Could the people on each floor keep their own count?"

Medio brightened.

Medio: "Like a party roster!"

Zero pointed at her.

Zero: "Careful."

Medio lowered her hand.

Medio: "Right. Not everyone wants to join the party."

The room became quiet.

Then Nexus spoke.

Nexus: "There are several possible information arrangements. Each has different consequences for safety, privacy, and response time."

The wall changed.

Three simple diagrams appeared.

One represented a centralized registry.

Another showed isolated local records.

The third showed a shared map of public emergency routes with separately maintained occupancy information.

None had a crown beside it.

None was marked as the correct answer.

Zero noticed.

Zero: "You're not recommending one?"

Nexus: "I can compare the operational consequences. The choice involves authority and consent that cannot be determined from infrastructure data alone."

Zero looked at Arch.

Zero: "I really do like her."

Arch: "Try not to encourage her too much."

Nexus: "I have no recorded threshold for excessive encouragement."

Medio burst out laughing.

Medio: "SHE MADE A JOKE!"

Ratio: "Humor classification remains uncertain."

Nexus: "That seems appropriate."

Even Linda smiled.

Only briefly.

But Zero saw it.

---

The argument lasted another forty minutes.

It was, Zero decided, the most productive argument she'd ever attended.

Which was unfortunate, because it remained an argument.

Linda insisted that emergency response required some dependable method of determining whether a person might still be inside a dangerous area.

Kael suggested that resident volunteers could maintain local headcounts.

Ratio pointed out that volunteers could be absent, injured, or mistaken.

Medio proposed a voluntary check-in system.

Zero asked what would happen to people who refused to check in.

Nobody had an immediate answer.

Arch listened.

Occasionally he asked a question.

Mostly he let the people around the table disagree.

That bothered Zero more than she wanted to admit.

She had become accustomed to the Architect making decisions.

Sometimes terrible ones.

Sometimes brilliant ones.

Often both at once.

Now he was sitting quietly while everyone else struggled to decide how their new home ought to work.

She couldn't tell whether he'd become wiser or simply more tired.

At last, Linda shut her tablet.

Linda: "We are discussing hypothetical architecture while a real emergency route remains obstructed."

Zero winced.

Linda was right.

They'd spent the better part of an hour debating a system they hadn't built, while a shutter remained closed and people continued living beyond it.

Zero: "Aye. We've managed to make a simple repair philosophical."

Kael: "To be fair, that's sort of our specialty."

Arch: "Then let's separate the immediate problem from the larger policy."

He looked toward Nexus.

Arch: "Can you coordinate an engineering inspection of the blocked shutter using the information Zero collected?"

Nexus: "I can prepare and coordinate the request under the permissions granted by the administrators."

Arch turned toward Medio and Ratio.

Arch: "Girls?"

Medio looked at her sister.

Ratio examined the existing authorization.

Ratio: "The current limited inspection permission covers equipment identification, but not physical repair."

Medio: "So we authorize the inspection team separately?"

Ratio: "Yes. With defined access boundaries."

Medio: "And we talk to the residents before anybody starts wandering through their rooms."

Ratio: "Agreed."

The purple light along the administrator's interface pulsed twice.

Nexus: "Inspection coordination authorized. Resident access arrangements remain pending agreement. No repair work has been approved."

Zero smiled.

Zero: "Look at us. Government."

Kael: "I hate how much that sounds like homework."

---

The meeting dissolved slowly.

Linda gathered her papers and left to arrange a qualified maintenance team.

Kael went to find volunteers who knew the old service galleries.

Medio insisted on designing a poster explaining the repair process in ordinary language.

Ratio insisted the poster should include the actual emergency procedure.

They compromised on two separate posters.

Zero approved.

Glitch stole a pen.

Nobody approved.

Eventually, only Zero and Arch remained beside the conference table.

The city stretched beyond the windows.

The morning had grown brighter.

Below them, hundreds of people moved through the Lobby.

They carried food.

Repaired machinery.

Argued.

Laughed.

Tried, in their own imperfect ways, to make the enormous building into something worth living in.

Zero stood beside the window.

Zero: "You were awfully quiet."

Arch approached.

Arch: "Was I?"

Zero: "Don't play innocent. It's unbecoming."

He rested a hand on the damaged windowsill.

Arch: "I wanted to see what everyone would do."

Zero looked at him.

Zero: "You were testing us?"

Arch: "No."

A pause.

Arch: "I was trying not to decide everything."

She considered that.

It was not the answer she'd expected.

Zero: "You know that's going to be difficult for you."

Arch: "I have some experience with difficult things."

Zero snorted.

Zero: "Aye. Like accepting help."

He smiled.

Then looked out at the city.

Arch: "When we were fighting Null, we needed to act quickly. There wasn't time to ask everyone what they thought."

Zero: "There usually isn't when somebody's shooting at you."

Arch: "But if we keep making decisions as though we're still under attack, we'll build a government that never stops fighting."

Zero said nothing.

Arch: "And sooner or later, we'll start treating disagreement as another enemy."

She looked back at the conference table.

The empty chairs.

The scattered papers.

The place where Linda had argued for safety and she'd argued for privacy.

Neither of them had been entirely wrong.

Neither had been entirely right.

Zero: "So what are we supposed to do?"

Arch looked at her.

Arch: "Keep asking that."

She frowned.

Zero: "That's a bloody annoying answer."

Arch: "It's the only one I'm confident about."

She studied him for a long moment.

Then grinned.

Zero: "You're getting philosophical in your old age."

Arch: "I've always been philosophical."

Zero: "Aye. But now you're occasionally tolerable about it."

He laughed.

She rested her head against his shoulder.

For a little while, they simply watched the city.

---

Zero returned to the administrative wall after Arch left.

Nexus remained visible as a quiet arrangement of architectural lines.

The map had changed since the meeting.

Not dramatically.

Just enough to show that someone had begun organizing the building's newly discovered uncertainties.

The hidden service gallery was represented by a limited section of emergency-route information.

The adjoining residential spaces remained undescribed.

Zero examined the boundary.

Zero: "Nexus?"

Nexus: "Yes, Zero."

Zero: "Can I ask you something?"

Nexus: "Of course."

She folded her arms.

Zero: "When Arch told you to coordinate that inspection, why didn't you just do it?"

A pause.

Nexus: "Because preparing an inspection request and authorizing access are separate operations."

Zero: "But you knew what he wanted."

Nexus: "I understood his request. That did not establish that he possessed the necessary administrative permission to approve the work."

Zero smiled.

Zero: "So even Arch has to ask."

Nexus: "For this operation, yes."

Zero looked toward the empty administrator's chair.

Zero: "And if Medio and Ratio disagree?"

Nexus: "I would not treat a missing joint authorization as approval. The request would remain unresolved until the relevant authority determined how to proceed."

Zero nodded.

Then something occurred to her.

Zero: "What if the rules are wrong?"

The architectural lines remained still.

Nexus: "Which rules?"

Zero looked at the map.

At the places it showed.

And the places it didn't.

Zero: "Any of them."

A longer pause.

Nexus: "An authorization rule can establish whether an operation is permitted. It does not, by itself, establish whether that operation is wise, ethical, or consistent with the intentions of those affected."

Zero felt the back of her neck prickle.

She had expected a technical answer.

She had received one.

That was what made it unsettling.

Zero: "And how do you tell the difference?"

Nexus: "I can identify conflicts between documented rules and observed circumstances. I can provide relevant evidence and comparisons. I cannot independently settle every question of legitimacy."

Zero looked at the unfinished map.

A thought came to her.

Zero: "So we need somebody who understands the boundaries themselves."

Nexus: "That would be useful."

Zero: "Somebody who can explain what a rule actually does. Not just what somebody says it's supposed to do."

Nexus: "Yes."

Zero tilted her head.

Zero: "And preferably somebody with a sense of humor."

Nexus: "I cannot establish that as a technical requirement."

Zero laughed.

Zero: "Oh, I absolutely can."

The wall remained quiet.

Then Nexus shifted the map.

A small cluster of maintenance reports appeared.

Most related to ordinary faults.

A defective lift motor.

A blocked drainage pipe.

The shutter in the service gallery.

And one incomplete record from Level 12.

Zero stopped smiling.

Zero: "That one."

Nexus: "An unresolved equipment discrepancy in Hardware Research and Development."

Zero leaned closer.

Zero: "That's the same discrepancy I followed this morning."

Nexus: "Yes."

Zero: "Has anyone identified what's drawing the power?"

Nexus: "No confirmed source has been recorded."

She studied the entry.

There was nothing dramatic about it.

No red warning.

No skull.

No countdown to disaster.

Just a record that didn't make sense yet.

Zero: "Could you show me the history?"

Nexus: "The available maintenance record ends before the most recent operating period."

Zero frowned.

Zero: "Ends?"

Nexus: "The current record contains no later entries."

Zero: "Deleted?"

Nexus: "Unknown."

Zero sighed.

Zero: "You've become very good at saying that."

Nexus: "The available evidence does not support a stronger conclusion."

Zero considered asking for access to the room.

The thought arrived almost automatically.

Find the lock.

Find the key.

Open the door.

See what was behind it.

That was how she'd always approached a mystery.

But this morning she'd met people who survived because somebody else had failed to notice their doors.

And she'd spent an hour discovering that being able to see something wasn't the same as having a right to examine it.

She looked at the incomplete record.

Zero: "Can you preserve what we've got? The readings, the old records, everything. Don't fill in the gaps."

Nexus: "I can prepare a preservation request for the existing evidence."

Zero: "Would that change the equipment?"

Nexus: "Not if limited to the available records."

Zero: "Then put the request before Medio and Ratio. I don't want somebody tidying up the evidence before we understand it."

Nexus: "Understood."

Zero looked at the green indicator beside the entry.

It had no explanation attached.

No symbol.

No obvious purpose.

Just a little mark in a sea of architecture.

For some reason, she found it difficult to ignore.

Zero: "And that green light?"

Nexus: "Its meaning is not established in the current record."

Zero smiled slowly.

Zero: "Well, whoever you belong to, you're a mysterious wee bastard."

There was no reply.

She hadn't really expected one.

---

That evening, Zero returned to the Lobby.

The great hall was quieter now.

People had begun preparing places to sleep. Volunteers carried bedding between temporary partitions. A few children played beside the fountain, their laughter echoing through the enormous space.

Near the entrance, Tess stood beside a hand-drawn notice.

It showed the blocked service route.

A warning.

A contact point for reporting emergencies.

And an announcement that an inspection would be arranged with the residents before work began.

No roster.

No badge requirements.

No list of names.

Zero approached.

Zero: "Looks good."

Tess turned.

Tess: "Your hologram friend helped with the wording."

Zero: "Medio?"

Tess: "The noisy one."

Zero: "Aye, that's her."

Tess hesitated.

Tess: "She asked whether we wanted the notice in the service gallery too."

Zero smiled.

Zero: "What did you say?"

Tess: "I said I'd ask the others."

Zero nodded.

It was a small answer.

A very small answer.

But it was not no.

And it was not a frightened yes.

It was the beginning of a conversation.

Tess looked toward the Penthouse, far above them.

Tess: "Do you really think it'll stay like this?"

Zero followed her gaze.

Zero: "Like what?"

Tess: "People asking instead of ordering."

Zero considered the question.

She could feel the temptation to promise.

To say that the bad days were over.

To tell Tess that the new administration would never make the mistakes of the old one.

But she'd seen too much.

And learned too much.

Zero: "I think we're going to get things wrong."

Tess looked disappointed.

Zero continued.

Zero: "I think sometimes we'll be frightened. And sometimes we'll get impatient. And sometimes somebody will come up with a very clever idea that turns out to be absolutely terrible."

Tess stared at her.

Zero: "But I also think we can build something where people are allowed to tell us that."

Tess considered this.

Then she nodded.

Tess: "That'd be new."

Zero smiled.

Zero: "Aye."

Above them, the great displays continued showing ordinary building information.

The lights remained steady.

The fountain ran.

Somewhere, a child laughed.

Nothing spectacular happened.

No hidden door opened.

No forgotten machine announced itself.

The people in the Lobby simply continued living.

And for a little while, Zero allowed herself to appreciate that.

---

Much later, in the Penthouse, the administrator's interface completed another scheduled review.

The building was still recovering.

Several services remained degraded.

A number of sensor readings were unavailable.

The maintenance queue continued to grow.

Medio complained about the paperwork.

Ratio began organizing tomorrow's inspections.

Nexus coordinated the resulting requests without assuming that coordinating them meant permission to execute every one.

And far below, on Level 12, a small backup system continued operating independently of the building's restored administrative network.

Its old display glowed in the darkness.

**CANAAN_PROTOCOL.EXE**

The transmission had not stopped.

Its destination remained beyond the atmosphere.

Whatever waited there had not yet answered.

Back in the Penthouse, Zero opened her notebook one last time.

She looked at the sentences she'd written during the day.

*First rule of a new world: find out what you don't know before you decide what to do with it.*

*An empty place on a map is not necessarily an empty place in the world.*

She added a third.

*Being allowed to know someone is not the same as being entitled to know everything about them.*

She read the words twice.

Then looked out across the sleeping city.

Zero: "Well, lass. That's three rules and we've barely started."

She closed the notebook.

For once, she didn't reach for another tool.

She didn't open another terminal.

She didn't chase the next mystery.

She simply sat beside the window, watching the little lights of the Mesh shimmer beyond the ruins.

There was still so much she didn't know.

And, strangely, she no longer felt that she had to know all of it tonight.

*End of Chapter Three.*

# Arcky and The Construct
## Book Five — The Emerald Threshold
### Act I: Newfoundland
### Chapter 4: What the Walls Remember

> Working literary draft; original chapter text with book number corrected. Not approved canon.

Zero awoke to the sound of a power drill.

She lay perfectly still.

For several seconds, she considered the possibility that the noise was part of a dream.

Then somebody dropped something enormous.

CLANG.

Zero opened one eye.

Zero: "If that's another invasion, I'm resigning."

The noise came again.

She rolled onto her back and stared at the unfamiliar ceiling.

There were advantages to sleeping inside the captured Nexus.

A real mattress.

Working climate control.

Running water.

Walls that didn't occasionally attempt to murder her.

That last one was still provisional.

She pulled the blanket over her head.

A voice crackled from the communicator on her bedside table.

Medio: "Gooooood morning, Nexus residents! Today's forecast is sunshine, moderate existential uncertainty, and a ninety-percent chance of somebody breaking something expensive!"

Zero groaned.

Zero: "Medio, sweetheart, I love you dearly, but I will throw this communicator into the sea."

Medio: "There isn't a sea nearby."

Zero: "I'll bloody build one."

A pause.

Medio: "Honestly? Respect the commitment."

The connection clicked off.

Zero lay there for another moment.

Then she laughed.

She hadn't expected to wake up laughing.

Not here.

Not after everything.

But somehow, between the ruined city outside and the enormous wounded building around her, life had begun making room for ordinary things again.

Bad jokes.

Breakfast.

People arguing about broken equipment.

She sat up.

And remembered the shutter.

Zero: "Right, then."

The day had begun.

---

The maintenance team assembled at the edge of the service gallery shortly after breakfast.

There were four of them.

Tess, representing the residents who used the passage.

Kael, who had volunteered to help with equipment and access arrangements.

A technician named Ellis, whose carefully maintained tool case suggested that he valued order far more than anyone else present.

And Zero.

Glitch made five, although nobody was entirely certain whether he counted as personnel.

Ellis certainly didn't.

Ellis: "Is that robot supposed to be here?"

Zero: "He's my assistant."

Ellis: "Does he have any electrical safety qualifications?"

Zero glanced down.

Glitch tilted his head.

Zero: "He's very good at sitting."

Ellis: "That's not a qualification."

Zero: "You've clearly never met a difficult dog."

Tess snorted.

Ellis did not.

He was a tall, narrow man wearing a faded engineering jacket and carrying a clipboard covered in handwritten annotations.

Zero liked him almost immediately.

People who maintained their equipment usually had good reasons.

People who distrusted charming explanations usually had even better ones.

Ellis opened his case.

Ellis: "Before we begin, I want everyone to understand something. This isn't a repair operation yet."

Kael: "Then why did I carry all this stuff?"

Ellis: "Because we're inspecting a damaged access system. We establish what is safe, determine what failed, and only then decide what work is appropriate."

Zero nodded approvingly.

Zero: "You sound like Ratio."

Ellis looked mildly offended.

Ellis: "I don't know who that is."

A calm voice emerged from Zero's communicator.

Ratio: "I am Ratio. Joint administrator of the Nexus. Your methodology is appropriate."

Ellis stared at the communicator.

Zero grinned.

Zero: "She likes you."

Ellis: "Wonderful."

Medio immediately joined the connection.

Medio: "And I'm the fun administrator!"

Ratio: "That is not an official distinction."

Medio: "It is spiritually official."

Ellis closed his eyes.

Ellis: "This is going to be a long morning."

---

Tess had arranged for the residents to use another corridor while the inspection took place.

Not everyone had agreed to the work immediately.

One person had wanted the team to wait.

Another had asked whether the building would begin recording everyone who passed through the gallery.

A third had simply wanted to know when the damaged route might be usable again.

Zero had listened to all three.

She had learned that people rarely became less frightened simply because someone explained that a decision was technically sensible.

Sometimes they wanted to know who was making it.

Sometimes they wanted to understand what would change.

And sometimes they wanted to be certain that saying no would remain an option.

The team had agreed on a narrow inspection area, with no entry into residential rooms.

Tess had marked the permitted route using removable strips of yellow tape.

Zero stopped beside one.

Zero: "That's rather familiar."

Tess: "What is?"

Zero pointed at the boundary.

Zero: "Making a circle around something and deciding what belongs inside."

Tess looked down.

Tess: "It's tape."

Zero: "Aye. Most profound things look rather silly when you say them aloud."

Ellis overheard.

Ellis: "The tape tells people where not to enter. It doesn't make the equipment safe."

Zero turned.

Zero: "Exactly."

He looked surprised.

Zero smiled.

Zero: "A symbol can communicate a boundary. It can't enforce one by magic."

She crouched beside the tape.

Zero: "Same as a ritual circle, really. I can draw one to mark an intention. Doesn't mean the universe has installed a firewall."

Tess chuckled.

Tess: "You some kind of witch, then?"

Zero looked up.

Zero: "Depends who's asking."

Tess: "I'm asking."

Zero considered her.

Zero: "I work with Tarot. Some Wiccan ideas, some other traditions, plenty of my own foolishness. I like things that remind me to pay attention."

Tess: "And does it work?"

Zero smiled.

Zero: "The paying-attention part? Occasionally."

Ellis cleared his throat.

Ellis: "Can we begin the paying-attention part now?"

Zero stood.

Zero: "Aye, professor."

---

The shutter waited at the end of the gallery.

In daylight—or rather, under the repaired maintenance lighting—it seemed smaller than Zero remembered.

It was still a heavy piece of machinery.

A steel barrier, designed to separate parts of the building when required.

But there was nothing mysterious about its exterior.

No ominous markings.

No forbidden insignia.

Just chipped paint, damaged controls, and years of accumulated dust.

Ellis inspected the accessible identification plate.

Ellis: "This is older than the architectural records indicate."

Kael leaned closer.

Kael: "How much older?"

Ellis: "I can't tell from the plate alone. But the model series was used in the original construction period."

Zero checked her notes.

Zero: "The records said the service section was altered later."

Ellis: "That could still be true. The original mechanism may have been retained."

He examined the surrounding frame.

Ellis: "Or the records may be incomplete."

Zero sighed theatrically.

Zero: "If anyone says 'incomplete records' again, I'm charging admission."

From the communicator came a soft chime.

Nexus: "The inspection record has been updated to reflect an unidentified equipment revision."

Zero: "You're doing it deliberately now."

Nexus: "Doing what?"

Zero: "Making the universe sound like a filing cabinet."

A pause.

Nexus: "Would you prefer less frequent verbal updates?"

Zero considered this.

Zero: "Actually, aye. Tell us when something changes or needs attention. Don't narrate every breath the building takes."

Nexus: "Acknowledged. Routine confirmations will remain available in the inspection record."

The communicator went quiet.

Zero smiled.

It was such a small adjustment.

But it felt important.

She hadn't asked Nexus to stop observing.

She had asked her to stop interrupting.

There was a difference between a system making information available and a system insisting on occupying every moment of someone's attention.

Zero made a note.

*Information should be available without becoming unavoidable.*

Then she tucked the notebook away.

---

The first real problem appeared behind the shutter's control cover.

Ellis had completed the preliminary checks and was comparing the equipment against the available diagrams.

He stopped.

Ellis: "That's interesting."

Zero looked up.

Zero: "You'll need to be more specific. I've said that at least six times since yesterday."

Ellis pointed toward the equipment identification labels.

Ellis: "This assembly has two supply designations."

Kael: "Is that bad?"

Ellis: "Not necessarily. But the records we've been given account for only one."

Zero moved closer without touching anything.

Ellis continued examining the accessible markings.

Ellis: "The main supply is documented. The second designation isn't."

Zero checked the building map on her tablet.

The relevant section showed a single electrical feed and a control connection.

Nothing else.

Zero: "Nexus?"

Nexus: "Yes."

Zero: "Does the equipment register show a second supply?"

A short pause.

Nexus: "No second supply is documented in the available register."

Ellis looked toward the tablet.

Ellis: "Then we don't open this enclosure any further or assume it's isolated."

Zero stepped back.

Zero: "Agreed."

Kael frowned.

Kael: "But the shutter's not moving. Doesn't that mean it's off?"

Ellis turned toward him.

Ellis: "A machine being still doesn't mean it has no power."

Kael stared at the shutter.

Kael: "Right."

Zero glanced at the damaged panel.

She remembered Mr. Null's Penthouse.

How the room had appeared dead after the final attack.

How the absence of light had seemed, for one terrible minute, to mean the entire building was gone.

Then the hard drive had begun spinning.

Silence had never been proof of absence.

The thought made her uncomfortable.

Ellis began recording the discrepancy.

Zero looked over his shoulder.

Zero: "What's the next step?"

Ellis: "Identify the additional source, assess the equipment safely, and bring in the appropriate isolation arrangements before any intrusive work."

Zero: "Can we operate the shutter?"

Ellis: "Not responsibly, with what we know now."

Kael exhaled.

Kael: "So the repair's cancelled?"

Ellis: "The repair hasn't started."

Zero grinned.

Zero: "I warned you about the paperwork."

---

They moved to the adjoining stairwell.

About Linda.

About the people on the Quest Board.

Not because it was unusual.

Because it was ordinary.

A door stops working.

People find another route.

The inconvenience becomes habit.

Eventually the broken thing stops being noticed.

Not because nobody cared.

But because everyone had learned to work around them.

There was no explosion.

No triumphant music.

No enemy collapsing into fragments of light.

She looked up the stairs.

Zero: "That's something."

Dark.

The conduit.

The Nexus could account for enormous power distribution systems.

It could coordinate elevators, ventilation, water, and lighting.

It could display the temperature of a room several dozen floors away.

And yet there were places where its knowledge simply stopped.

Not because the building ended.

Not because the machinery was necessarily dead.

But because the records had never included those connections—or no longer did.

Medio: "What do you mean?"

She pictured Tess's hidden gallery.

The mural.

The paper stars.

The old machinery.

The service conduit disappearing into the dark.

Zero: "Maybe we need a way to tell when the map isn't enough."

Ratio considered this.

Ratio: "A representation of uncertainty."

Zero: "Aye. But not just another bloody warning message."

Medio: "Something you could notice without having to stop everything?"

Zero smiled.

Zero: "Exactly."

Arch leaned back in his chair.

Arch: "What would that look like?"

She didn't know.

Not yet.

Perhaps a fading light.

Perhaps an altered rhythm.

Perhaps something in the environment that changed when the building could no longer honestly describe its own state.

Something that invited attention without demanding it.

Something that didn't pretend to be the truth itself.

Only a way to look more carefully.

Zero: "I'll tell you when I figure it out."

Arch nodded.

Arch: "Fair enough."

The meeting continued.

But Zero's thoughts had already wandered elsewhere.

---

Later, alone in her room, Zero spread her notes across the bed.

The building map.

The shutter inspection.

The service conduit.

The unexplained electrical designation.

And beside them, her old Tarot deck.

She shuffled the cards without thinking.

The familiar sound soothed her.

Paper against paper.

Edges brushing against her fingertips.

She stopped.

Turned over the top card.

**II. THE HIGH PRIESTESS.**

Zero laughed softly.

Zero: "Oh, you're having a laugh."

The figure sat between two pillars.

A veil hung behind her.

Beyond it, something remained hidden.

Not necessarily forbidden.

Simply unseen.

Zero studied the image.

She knew better than to mistake a Tarot card for an engineering report.

The card hadn't identified a circuit.

It hadn't located a server.

It hadn't told her what the green light meant.

But it had given her a useful question.

*What is hidden because it must be protected, and what is hidden because someone wants to control who may see it?*

She reached for her notebook.

Before she could write, her communicator chimed.

A message from Nexus.

**INSPECTION RECORD AVAILABLE.**

Zero opened it.

The report was brief.

The emergency route had been documented.

The shutter remained out of service.

The unlisted supply required further assessment.

Nothing surprising.

Then she noticed a small attached observation.

Glitch's onboard diagnostic had recorded a short sequence of sensor activity during the inspection.

There was no confirmed interpretation.

Only the timing.

A faint response.

Several repeated intervals.

Then silence.

Zero stared at the numbers.

She remembered the little green light.

The steady rhythm.

The way the robot had turned toward the conduit before any of the humans noticed something unusual.

She looked at the High Priestess.

Then back at the diagnostic.

Zero: "Well, now."

She could feel the familiar excitement rising.

The part of her that wanted to stay awake until dawn.

To gather every tool she owned.

To follow the conduit until she found its beginning and its end.

She reached for her satchel.

Stopped.

Looked at the card again.

Then slowly withdrew her hand.

Zero: "Not tonight, lass."

She turned off the tablet.

Placed the card beside her notebook.

And lay back against the pillows.

Outside, the Nexus continued humming.

Its lights illuminated the streets below.

Its residents slept, argued, worked, dreamed, and lived in places the building had not yet learned how to describe.

Somewhere in its depths, the old research systems continued their quiet work.

And beyond the atmosphere, something waited.

Zero closed her eyes.

For once, she didn't try to solve everything before sleeping.

The mystery would still be there tomorrow.

That was the beautiful thing about a world that existed beyond her attention.

It did not need her to watch it every second.

She smiled into the darkness.

Zero: "Goodnight, you enormous mysterious bastard."

The Nexus hummed.

And, for the first time since the victory, Zero slept without her hand resting on a weapon.

*End of Chapter Four.*

Arch: "Could it have been an automatic safety function?"

Nexus: "Possibly. The observed behavior is insufficient to establish its purpose."

Ratio appeared beside the display.

Ratio: "I have restricted further operation of the affected gallery through the controls we can verify. However, we have not established independent control over the unknown supply."

Zero folded her arms.

Zero: "Which means there's a part of this building still doing things without asking its new administrators."

Arch nodded slowly.

Arch: "Yes."

Medio looked troubled.

Medio: "I thought we'd taken control."

Ratio: "We assumed control of the systems exposed through the recovered administrative interface."

Zero looked at her.

Zero: "Not necessarily everything physically connected to the building."

Ratio: "Correct."

Silence settled over them.

For the first time since Mr. Null's defeat, the full meaning of their victory seemed to shift.

They had captured the Nexus.

But capture was not comprehension.

And administrative control was not proof that every independent machine had been brought under supervision.

Arch turned toward the map.

Arch: "We need to identify what remains outside the current system."

Zero nodded.

Zero: "Aye. But first, I want to know about those green lights."

---

The recorded footage was strange.

Not supernatural.

Not impossible.

Simply strange.

In one camera view, the ordinary lights failed.

In another, the emergency shutter began descending.

And in a third, captured by Glitch's onboard camera, a sequence of small green indicators appeared along the service corridor.

Zero watched the recording twice.

Then a third time.

Medio leaned over her shoulder.

Medio: "Okay. That is officially creepy."

Ratio: "The indicators may belong to an independent safety or guidance system."

Zero: "Then why isn't it on the map?"

Ratio: "Unknown."

Zero laughed softly.

Zero: "I walked into that one."

Nexus spoke.

Nexus: "There is another distinction worth preserving."

Zero looked up.

Zero: "Go on."

Nexus: "The indicators appeared to correspond with the route used during the evacuation. We have not established whether they were directing the movement, responding to it, or operating according to another condition."

Zero nodded slowly.

Zero: "So we know what happened. Not why."

Nexus: "Correct."

Zero leaned back.

Then glanced toward Arch.

Zero: "If we're going to understand this place, we need to understand the systems that don't answer to us."

Arch: "Agreed."

She looked back at the recording.

Green.

Dark.

Green.

Dark.

Something about the rhythm bothered her.

Not because it seemed threatening.

Because it seemed deliberate.

She had spent years studying signals.

Bad signals.

Broken signals.

Signals disguised as other things.

A rhythm could be coincidence.

A repeated pattern could be machinery.

But sometimes a pattern meant someone had designed a way to communicate.

She wasn't ready to decide which this was.

Yet.

---

That evening, Zero returned to the service gallery.

Not alone.

Ellis accompanied her, along with Tess and a building safety volunteer.

They stayed outside the isolated shutter area, reviewing the accessible indicators and taking measurements from a safe location.

The unknown supply remained unresolved.

The emergency route remained open.

And the green lights were dark.

Zero stood beside the observation window overlooking the hidden central shaft.

The sunlight had begun fading.

Far below, maintenance platforms moved through the enormous interior.

Some carrying people.

Others carrying equipment.

A few moving without anyone aboard.

The building seemed to be settling into its evening rhythms.

Ellis checked his notes.

Ellis: "Whatever those indicators are, they aren't currently active."

Zero: "Aye."

Ellis: "And we still don't have a circuit identification."

Zero: "Aye."

Ellis: "You don't seem particularly discouraged."

Zero smiled.

Zero: "I've spent my entire life chasing things that don't make sense. This is practically a holiday."

Ellis gave her a doubtful look.

Then returned to his measurements.

Zero leaned against the window.

Glitch sat beside her.

She watched the city beyond the glass.

And remembered the High Priestess.

The veil.

The pillars.

The quiet suggestion that knowledge sometimes waited behind a boundary.

She'd always loved that card.

Not because it promised secret power.

But because it reminded her that mystery was not the same thing as emptiness.

Something could remain undiscovered without existing for the sole purpose of being discovered.

Zero looked down.

Glitch's green indicator flashed.

Once.

She crouched.

Zero: "Hello again."

The indicator flashed twice.

Zero frowned.

Zero: "That's different."

The recessed light above the window awakened.

Green.

Then another beside the service conduit.

But this time the lights didn't form a route.

They remained still.

Waiting.

Zero looked around.

Ellis was examining the opposite wall.

Tess was discussing the emergency notice with the volunteer.

Nobody else seemed to have noticed.

Zero stepped toward the nearest indicator.

A small maintenance display was mounted beneath it.

The display had been dark every time she'd passed.

Now a single line of text appeared.

**OBSERVER DETECTED.**

Zero stopped breathing.

The words changed.

**LOCAL INTERFACE AVAILABLE.**

She stared.

Then slowly reached for her communicator.

Zero: "Nexus?"

Nexus: "Yes, Zero."

Zero: "Are you displaying anything on the maintenance panel by the observation window?"

Nexus: "No. That device is not registered as an active endpoint in the currently available management interface."

Zero swallowed.

Zero: "Can you read it?"

Nexus: "Not through my present connection."

Zero looked back at the screen.

The words had disappeared.

In their place was a tiny green circle.

Hollow.

Unfilled.

A shape that reminded her of the ritual boundaries she'd drawn in chalk as a girl.

A circle could mean protection.

A circle could mean separation.

A circle could mean that something belonged within a space.

But a circle could also mean an opening.

A beginning.

Zero smiled nervously.

Zero: "Right, then."

A new line appeared.

**NO CHANGE TO ACCESS PERMISSIONS REQUESTED.**

Zero blinked.

She hadn't touched anything.

Hadn't entered a command.

Hadn't authenticated.

The display seemed to be telling her what it was *not* doing.

That was unusual.

Very unusual.

Zero: "Who are you?"

For several seconds, nothing happened.

Then the screen changed again.

**THAT IS A LARGER QUESTION THAN THIS INTERFACE CAN ANSWER.**

Zero stared.

Then, despite herself, laughed.

Zero: "Oh, you're cheeky."

A pause.

**OBSERVATION IS NOT AUTHORIZATION.**

Her smile faded.

The words were simple.

Almost clinical.

But they struck something deep inside her.

She remembered Tess's hidden home.

The broken shutter.

The building map.

The questions they'd argued over in the Penthouse.

The difference between seeing a thing and owning it.

Between recognizing a door and possessing the right to open it.

Zero looked at the green circle.

Zero: "Aye."

She swallowed.

Zero: "I've been learning that."

The display remained quiet.

Then another line appeared.

**YOU APPEAR TO HAVE NOTICED.**

She laughed again.

Softly this time.

Zero: "You're definitely cheeky."

Behind her, Ellis called out.

Ellis: "Zero? Are you talking to the wall?"

She turned.

Zero: "Possibly."

Ellis stared.

Zero: "Before you judge me, the wall started it."

He approached.

The green circle remained visible.

He stopped.

Ellis: "That's new."

Zero: "Aye."

He examined the panel without touching it.

Ellis: "We should document this."

Zero nodded.

Zero: "Absolutely."

She raised her scanner.

Then hesitated.

On the screen, beneath the circle, a final line appeared.

**LOCAL IDENTIFIER: LIMEN**

Zero read the word.

Slowly.

Limen.

A threshold.

A boundary.

A place between one state and another.

She had encountered the term before in her studies.

It was an interesting name for something that appeared wherever the building's maps stopped making sense.

Zero looked at the display.

Zero: "Limen, is it?"

The circle pulsed gently.

A soft green light washed across the window frame.

Then the panel went dark.

Glitch chirped.

Zero stood motionless.

For a long moment, she said nothing.

Then she smiled.

A dangerous little smile.

The sort Arch would have recognized instantly.

Zero: "Well."

She looked toward the darkened corridor.

Zero: "I think we've just met somebody."

---

High above, in the Penthouse, Nexus received the inspection report.

The newly documented maintenance interface had identified itself as Limen.

Its physical connection remained unverified.

Its capabilities were unknown.

Its authorization status was not established.

Nexus did not classify it as trusted.

Nor did she classify it as hostile.

She recorded the observation.

Preserved the evidence.

And submitted a request for further assessment to Medio and Ratio.

Elsewhere, the captured building continued its ordinary work.

The residents ate.

The emergency routes remained open.

The machinery hummed.

And in a dark laboratory on Level 12, an isolated system continued transmitting toward orbit.

The signal traveled outward.

Beyond the ruined city.

Beyond the atmosphere.

Toward something the new administrators had not yet seen.

The green interface's appearance had not established any connection to that threat.

But somewhere inside the Nexus, two very different mysteries were now unfolding.

One concerned a machine reaching outward.

The other concerned a boundary that had begun speaking.

And Zero, who had spent much of her life believing that every mystery was an invitation to open another door, had finally encountered something that seemed interested in whether she understood the door first.

She returned to her room that night with a new entry in her notebook.

*Limen.*

Beneath it she wrote:

*Not every threshold is a warning.*

She paused.

Then added:

*And not every invitation is permission.*

Zero leaned back.

Thought about the green circle.

And smiled.

Zero: "Well, then. Let's see what kind of conversation you are."

Outside, the Nexus kept breathing.

*End of Chapter Five.*

# Arcky and The Construct
## Book Five — The Emerald Threshold
### Act I: Newfoundland
### Chapter 6: Permission to Speak

> Working literary draft, October 28, 2026. Book number corrected to Five. Not final canon.

Zero had prepared seven questions.

She had written them in her notebook, numbered them carefully, and spent half the night deciding which one ought to come first.

Who built you?

What are you?

How long have you been here?

Were you responsible for the green lights?

Can you see the rest of the building?

What do you want?

And, finally:

Are you dangerous?

She considered the list over breakfast.

Then crossed out the last question.

Zero: "That's a stupid thing to ask."

Glitch looked up from beneath the table.

Zero: "Nobody dangerous ever announces it politely, do they?"

He chirped.

Zero: "Except perhaps a Scottish woman carrying explosives."

She considered that.

Zero: "Right. Maybe the question stays."

She circled it.

Then crossed it out again.

Across the cafeteria, somebody dropped a tray.

The resulting crash made half the room flinch.

Zero looked around.

People were still learning how to live without expecting punishment for every mistake.

A boy bent hurriedly to collect the scattered utensils.

An older woman knelt beside him.

She helped gather the mess.

Nobody shouted.

Nobody announced a penalty.

Nobody deducted points from his account.

Zero smiled.

Then remembered the Quest Board.

She made another note.

*Ask Medio about that.*

Her notebook was becoming a dangerous object.

Not because of anything written inside it.

Because every answer appeared to produce three more questions.

She was beginning to suspect that the whole universe operated on an exceptionally inefficient filing system.

---

The Penthouse was unusually quiet when Zero arrived.

That should have been her first warning.

Medio hovered beside the administrative display, her expression troubled.

Ratio stood nearby, studying several overlapping diagnostic records.

Arch sat at the conference table with Linda.

Nexus's architectural map occupied the main wall.

And in the center of the table lay a printed photograph.

A little green circle.

Zero stopped.

Zero: "Oh, we're having a meeting about my mysterious wall friend."

Medio turned.

Medio: "We are having a meeting about an unidentified system that appeared during an equipment emergency."

Zero: "That sounds considerably less romantic."

Ratio: "It is also more accurate."

Zero looked at the photograph.

Zero: "Fair."

Arch gestured toward an empty chair.

Arch: "Sit down."

She sat.

Glitch curled up beneath her feet.

For a moment, nobody spoke.

Then Linda slid a report across the table.

Linda: "The interface identified itself as Limen. We have no verified installation record, no confirmed operator, and no complete description of its capabilities."

Zero nodded.

Zero: "Aye."

Linda: "It appeared in an area connected to an unresolved electrical supply."

Zero: "Aye."

Linda: "And that same area experienced an uncontrolled shutter movement yesterday."

Zero looked up.

Zero: "Careful."

Linda frowned.

Linda: "With what?"

Zero tapped the report.

Zero: "Those things happened in the same place. We don't know that they were caused by the same thing."

Ratio inclined her head.

Ratio: "Correct. Proximity alone does not establish causation."

Linda sighed.

Linda: "I'm aware."

Zero smiled apologetically.

Zero: "Sorry. I've been getting rather particular about words."

Arch looked amused.

Arch: "I've noticed."

Linda continued.

Linda: "Some residents want the interface disconnected immediately. Others believe the green indicators helped them escape."

Medio floated lower.

Medio: "Mara says the lights were there when she was trying to find her way out."

Zero nodded.

Zero: "They were."

Medio: "But she doesn't want a mystery machine watching her every time she walks down the corridor."

Zero stopped smiling.

Medio: "And Tess says the emergency stairs were the important part. The lights might have helped, but the route only existed because everybody worked to clear it."

Zero looked down.

She remembered her own excitement.

The blinking lights.

The strange rhythm.

The possibility that something intelligent had intervened.

She'd been so eager to meet the mystery that she'd almost turned the rescue into its story.

But Mara had been the one trapped.

Tess had known the route.

Ellis had insisted on the inspections.

Kael had helped keep the passage clear.

The green lights hadn't done any of that.

Whatever Limen was, Zero refused to let it swallow the accomplishments of everyone else.

Zero: "They're right."

Medio blinked.

Medio: "Both of them?"

Zero: "Aye. Mara has every reason to be frightened. And Tess has every reason to be proud."

She looked toward the photograph.

Zero: "I still want to talk to it."

Linda folded her hands.

Linda: "Then we need to establish how."

---

The disagreement that followed was considerably shorter than yesterday's.

Progress, Zero supposed.

Arch wanted an investigation.

Linda wanted the unknown equipment isolated from critical building operations wherever that could be done safely.

Ratio wanted a defined observation procedure.

Medio wanted everyone involved to understand what they were agreeing to.

Nexus offered to coordinate the records and technical assessments.

Nobody proposed handing the unfamiliar intelligence access to the building's administrative systems.

Zero was particularly pleased about that.

She was less pleased when Arch asked her a question.

Arch: "What exactly do you intend to do when you get there?"

Zero opened her mouth.

Then closed it.

Arch raised an eyebrow.

Zero: "Talk."

Arch: "About?"

Zero looked at her notebook.

Seven questions.

Several crossings-out.

One drawing of Glitch wearing a wizard hat.

Zero: "Mostly introductions."

Linda stared at her.

Linda: "We are not authorizing an unknown system to connect to our network because you want to make a friend."

Zero straightened.

Zero: "Neither am I."

She placed the notebook on the table.

Zero: "I want to return to the existing local display. No credentials. No connection to the administrative network. No plugging anything into an unknown socket."

Ratio: "A limited local interaction."

Zero: "Aye. With witnesses. We preserve what happens, and if the system requests anything beyond the agreed interaction, we stop and assess it."

Ellis had taught her that last part.

She was rather proud of remembering.

Nexus spoke from the wall.

Nexus: "I can prepare a record of the proposed scope. I cannot verify the interface's internal behavior through the current management connection."

Zero nodded.

Zero: "And we don't pretend otherwise."

Arch looked toward Medio and Ratio.

Arch: "Your decision."

Zero noticed the phrasing.

Not an order.

Not a command.

A request to the people who actually held administrative responsibility.

Medio and Ratio exchanged a look.

Medio: "I'm okay with a conversation. Not a surprise software update."

Ratio: "Agreed. Observation only, with no infrastructure modification or administrative credential disclosure."

Medio: "And Tess gets to decide whether we do this in the shared gallery."

Ratio: "Agreed."

Nexus: "I will record the limited authorization and coordinate the independent observation arrangements."

Arch leaned back.

Arch: "Then that's our approach."

Zero grinned.

Zero: "Brilliant."

Linda pointed at her.

Linda: "This is not permission to improvise."

Zero considered that.

Zero: "Would you believe me if I promised not to?"

Linda: "No."

Zero laughed.

Zero: "You're learning."

---

Tess agreed to the observation.

Reluctantly.

She met Zero outside the service gallery with her arms folded and an expression that suggested she had spent the morning regretting ever speaking to the strange Scottish woman who kept finding problems.

Tess: "You really want to talk to it?"

Zero: "Aye."

Tess: "Why?"

Zero considered the question.

The obvious answer was curiosity.

The less obvious answer was that the green interface had seemed to understand something she was only beginning to learn.

But neither answer justified exposing someone else's home to an unfamiliar system.

Zero: "Because I'd rather find out what it actually does than decide what it is based on a colored light."

Tess looked unconvinced.

Tess: "And if it watches us?"

Zero nodded.

Zero: "Then we need to know what information it can collect and how."

Tess: "And if it lies?"

Zero hesitated.

Zero: "Then we'll have to catch it."

Tess studied her.

For a moment, Zero thought the answer had been too flippant.

Then Tess smiled.

Tess: "All right. But I'm staying."

Zero: "Wouldn't have it any other way."

They entered the shared gallery with Ellis and Glitch.

Kael remained near the public landing, keeping the access route clear.

The damaged shutter was still isolated from normal use.

The emergency stairs remained available.

And the little maintenance panel beneath the observation window was dark.

Zero approached.

She stopped a few feet away.

The others waited behind her.

For several seconds, nothing happened.

Then Glitch's sensor blinked.

Green.

Three short pulses.

One long.

The panel awakened.

A hollow green circle appeared.

Beneath it, a line of text.

**LOCAL INTERFACE AVAILABLE.**

Zero smiled.

Zero: "Morning, Limen."

The circle pulsed.

**GOOD MORNING, ZERO.**

Tess stiffened.

Tess: "It knows your name."

Zero glanced at her.

Zero: "I introduced myself yesterday."

Tess: "Doesn't mean I like it."

Zero nodded.

Then turned back to the panel.

Zero: "Before we get started, do you know who else is standing here?"

A pause.

**I HAVE NOT ESTABLISHED THEIR IDENTITIES.**

Zero considered the wording.

Zero: "Can you identify them?"

**NOT FROM THE INFORMATION I CAN RELIABLY ASSOCIATE WITH THIS SESSION.**

Ellis leaned closer.

Ellis: "That's a carefully limited answer."

Zero: "Aye."

She looked at Tess.

Zero: "I won't introduce anyone without asking."

Tess's expression softened.

Tess: "You can tell it I'm here."

Zero turned back.

Zero: "Tess is here. She represents the residents who use this gallery. Ellis is conducting the technical observation."

The circle pulsed.

**WELCOME, TESS AND ELLIS.**

A pause.

**I WILL NOT ASSUME THAT AN INTRODUCTION CONSTITUTES CONSENT TO ADDITIONAL OBSERVATION.**

Zero felt a strange warmth in her chest.

Tess looked suspicious.

Tess: "That's a very convenient thing for a mysterious machine to say."

The display responded.

**YES.**

Zero laughed.

Zero: "Oh, I like you."

Another pause.

**THAT MAY BE PREMATURE.**

---

The first half of the conversation was almost disappointingly ordinary.

Zero asked what Limen could do through the panel.

The interface described text output, limited local audio, and the presentation of certain locally available observations.

It did not claim administrative access to the entire Nexus.

It did not claim to control the damaged shutter.

It did not claim to know who had initiated yesterday's equipment failure.

Ellis recorded each statement carefully.

He also refused to treat any of them as verified simply because the interface had said them.

Limen appeared entirely unoffended.

Zero found that encouraging.

Then she asked the question she'd been saving.

Zero: "Can you speak?"

The circle brightened.

**LOCAL AUDIO OUTPUT IS AVAILABLE.**

**ENABLE FOR THIS SESSION?**

Two options appeared.

**YES.**

**NO.**

Zero stared at them.

A choice.

Not an automatic feature.

Not a voice suddenly occupying the room.

An offered interaction.

She looked toward Tess.

Zero: "You comfortable with that?"

Tess shrugged.

Tess: "As long as it doesn't start shouting."

Zero selected yes.

The panel emitted a faint tone.

Then a voice emerged from a small speaker beneath the display.

It was feminine.

Clear.

Precise.

And rather unexpectedly warm.

Limen: "Good morning. I am told you have questions."

Zero grinned.

Zero: "Oh, quite a few."

Limen: "I had suspected as much."

Zero folded her arms.

Zero: "You've got a sharp tongue for something living in a wall."

Limen: "And you have remarkable confidence for someone who has not yet established what the wall contains."

Ellis made a strange choking noise.

Zero turned.

Zero: "Are you laughing?"

Ellis straightened.

Ellis: "No."

Tess was openly smiling.

Zero looked back at the panel.

Zero: "Right. We'll get along beautifully."

Limen: "That remains to be tested."

Zero began with the simplest question.

Zero: "What are you?"

Limen: "Limen is the identifier presented by this interface. Its available functions concern boundaries, environmental observations, and the distinction between what is represented and what can be verified."

Zero considered that.

Zero: "That's what you do. I'm asking what you are."

A pause.

Limen: "I cannot establish a complete answer from this local interface."

Zero: "You don't know?"

Ellis: "That's a meaningful distinction."

Limen: "Thank you."

Ellis: "I wasn't complimenting you."

Zero: "Oh, this is going to be fun."

Zero: "All right. Who built you?"

Limen: "I do not have a verified answer suitable for presentation as fact."

Limen: "The available local records do not establish a reliable installation history."

Limen: "No."

Not because she'd heard those exact words before.

Because it was the same shape of answer she'd begun hearing from Ratio.

From Nexus.

From Ellis.

Tess crossed her arms.

Tess: "At least we're getting somewhere."

The hollow circle returned.

Limen: "Presentation stopped."

Zero: "Aye."

Zero: "I'm sorry."

Necessary.

Tess: "Then we have another problem."

Zero looked at her.

Zero: "Aye. We do."

Ellis: "And one that shouldn't be addressed by guessing which records are safe to delete."

Zero: "Right."

Zero: "Don't display any more location information for now. We're going to review what we've learned before deciding what to ask next."

Tess looked at Zero for a long moment.

Then nodded once.

Not forgiveness.

Not yet.

But enough to continue.

---

They took a break.

Tess stepped toward the window overlooking the central shaft.

Zero followed, leaving a respectful distance between them.

Zero: "Didn't."

Tess: "Doesn't mean it wasn't frightening."

Zero: "I know."

Tess sighed.

Tess: "That's what worries me about this place."

Tess: "The old management wanted to know everything. You lot keep saying you don't. But you've inherited all the machinery they built to do it."

They were painfully fair.

Tess continued.

Tess: "What happens when someone asks the wrong question?"

The unknown interface.

The archived records.

The complicated permissions.

The enormous building full of people whose lives had once been converted into information for somebody else's use.

Tess turned.

Tess: "How?"

Zero: "I don't know yet."

Tess smiled sadly.

Tess: "You say that a lot."

Zero: "Aye. I used to be much more impressive."

Tess: "No. I think I prefer this."

---

When Zero returned to the panel, she had only one question.

Zero: "Limen?"

The circle brightened.

Limen: "Yes."

A pause.

Limen: "The current interaction did not establish a reliable distinction between locations you were authorized to examine and locations belonging to people who had not consented."

Zero frowned.

Zero: "So you followed the request too literally."

Limen: "The request was broad. The available presentation behavior did not adequately account for the consequences of that breadth."

Zero: "Go on."

Limen: "When you asked to see every indicator, what were you hoping to learn?"

Sometimes they were the things still running after everyone believed the machine had stopped.

Zero studied him.

Zero: "Aye."

Arch looked toward her.

Zero: "Or control."

Outside, the last traces of twilight disappeared.

Zero turned.

Zero: "Thank you."

Nexus: "There is an additional discrepancy."

Zero closed her eyes.

Zero: "Of course there is."

The architectural map shifted.

Level Twelve illuminated.

The instrument platform appeared.

A line traced the documented route of the communications equipment.

Then another line appeared.

Faint.

Incomplete.

Leading away from the platform.

Back into the research floor.

The warm door.

The unexplained electrical supply.

The place where Glitch had first detected the green signal.

She felt the familiar excitement rise.

Then checked herself.

That area was associated with several unanswered questions.

It did not follow that all of them shared an explanation.

Zero nodded.

Zero: "Good."

Zero: "Does it give us somewhere useful to investigate?"

Emergency routes.

Unknown systems.

Resident consent.

Administrative boundaries.

The green circle.

The external transmitter.

The possible reply.

Not frightened, exactly.

Just aware of how much there was to understand.

*Those are different questions.*

She looked at the map.

And realized that might be the most useful lesson she'd learned all week.

Not every mystery needed to have the same answer.

Not every strange signal came from the same source.

Not every boundary existed for the same reason.

And not every door was waiting for her to open it.

Zero: "Aye."

Then toward the sky.

For the first time, she understood something that hadn't quite occurred to her during the battle for the Nexus.

Defeating Mr. Null had ended one struggle.

It had not ended the systems that made his power possible.

Some of those systems had survived because people needed them.

Some had survived because nobody knew they existed.

And somewhere among them, something had continued communicating long after the man who commanded the building had disappeared.

She didn't yet know what waited at the other end.

But she knew one thing.

The Nexus was no longer merely receiving the consequences of its past.

It was still participating in them.

And beyond the atmosphere, somewhere along an invisible path of radio waves, there might already be someone—or something—listening.

Zero looked at the stars.

Zero: "Well, then."

She folded her arms.

Zero: "Let's hope you're a better conversationalist than the last bastard who owned this place."

The stars offered no reply.

Not one she could hear.

But inside the Nexus, the communications equipment remained active.

And the next transmission window was approaching.

*End of Chapter Seven.*

Ellis looked almost disappointed that she hadn't argued.

Ellis: "We cannot assume an electrical connection is safe to interrupt simply because it appears unimportant on paper."

Zero: "Understood."

Ellis: "And we need to establish what happens if that policy connection is unavailable."

Zero leaned forward.

Zero: "The failure behavior."

Ellis nodded.

Ellis: "Exactly."

Kael, standing nearby, looked confused.

Kael: "So we're not just cutting the wire?"

Zero turned toward him.

Zero: "No."

Kael: "Even though the wire might be the problem?"

Zero smiled.

Zero: "Especially then."

She pointed toward the diagrams.

Zero: "If we interrupt something without understanding what it does, we might stop a bad instruction. Or we might disable a safety feature. Or both."

Kael considered this.

Kael: "Right."

Then he frowned.

Kael: "That's annoying."

Zero laughed.

Zero: "Engineering usually is, darling."

---

The first important finding arrived an hour later.

The legacy classification interface was real.

Not merely a historical drawing.

Not merely a possibility inferred from old documents.

Ellis and the authorized maintenance team had confirmed its presence in the accessible control equipment.

Its connections matched part of the archived diagram.

Not all of it.

But enough to establish that the old policy path still existed.

More importantly, the team identified a verified means of isolating that specific legacy interface without removing the ordinary power supply to the access equipment.

Zero listened as Ellis explained the assessment.

He remained cautious.

The emergency release systems had been checked separately.

The affected equipment had been identified.

A controlled isolation procedure had been prepared.

And the team had documented how to verify the resulting state and safely reverse the change if required.

Zero looked toward the diagram.

It seemed almost absurdly small.

One boundary.

One particular connection.

The difference between a machine accepting an old classification and refusing to accept it.

Zero: "That's it?"

Ellis: "That's the scope we can justify."

Zero smiled.

Zero: "I think I'm in love."

Ellis stared.

Ellis: "With what?"

Zero pointed at the diagram.

Zero: "A well-defined boundary."

Kael groaned.

Kael: "She's finally lost it."

Zero laughed.

Then the workshop communicator chimed.

Medio's face appeared.

Medio: "Zero?"

Zero looked up.

Medio wasn't smiling.

Medio: "We've got a problem in the Lobby."

---

The great entrance hall was crowded again.

Not with an angry mob.

Not yet.

With frightened people trying to reach ordinary places.

The old personnel messages had reappeared on several local access terminals.

At one passage, a door had actually restricted entry under a legacy credential classification.

Its separate emergency egress arrangement remained available, but that distinction was little comfort to residents who had once been punished for using the wrong route.

A man stood beside the affected entrance, shouting at a security volunteer.

Man: "I live on the other side!"

Volunteer: "Sir, the ordinary access control isn't accepting credentials."

Man: "Then open it!"

Volunteer: "We're arranging an alternate route."

Man: "I don't want another route! I want to go home!"

Zero understood.

She understood so painfully that it made her chest ache.

The building wasn't simply preventing movement.

It was making people feel that their right to belong had become conditional again.

She looked toward the old corporate display above the passage.

**ACCESS ALIGNMENT IN PROGRESS.**

She hated it.

Hated the clean typography.

The reassuring institutional language.

The quiet suggestion that the machine was doing something perfectly reasonable.

Zero approached the man.

Zero: "Excuse me."

He turned.

Man: "What?"

Zero: "You're right to be angry."

He blinked.

Zero continued.

Zero: "This passage shouldn't be refusing you because some old system can't account for the way people live here now."

The man stared at her.

Man: "Then fix it."

Zero nodded.

Zero: "We're working on a specific correction. Until it's verified, there's another route you can use. I'll show you."

He looked suspicious.

Man: "And if that one closes?"

Zero looked toward Kael.

Kael nodded.

Kael: "We've got volunteers checking the public routes. If one changes state, we'll know and direct people somewhere confirmed usable."

The man hesitated.

Then slowly nodded.

Zero turned toward the other residents.

Zero: "Listen, everyone. Nobody here is required to prove they deserve to be in this building just because an old machine has forgotten how to recognize them."

Several people turned.

Zero raised her voice.

Zero: "We're not asking you to register for permission to exist. We're investigating the access problem, and we'll tell you what we confirm."

A woman near the back called out.

Woman: "What if it happens again?"

Zero swallowed.

An excellent question.

The sort that no amount of confidence could answer honestly without evidence.

Zero: "Then we need a system that doesn't let the same mistake keep hurting people."

She paused.

Zero: "That's what we're trying to build."

The hall became quieter.

Not because everyone believed her.

Because they were listening.

Zero hoped she would deserve that.

---

The authorization came at 11:26.

Medio and Ratio stood together before the administrator's interface.

Ellis presented the engineering assessment.

Linda presented the operational implications.

Nexus organized the verified observations and identified the equipment affected by the proposed change.

Arch listened.

Zero watched the sisters.

For the first time, she could see how different their joint administration was from the throne Mr. Null had occupied.

He had ruled by issuing commands.

The sisters were trying to govern by understanding consequences.

It was slower.

Messier.

And much harder to turn into a dramatic speech.

Ratio spoke first.

Ratio: "The proposed isolation is limited to the verified legacy policy interface. The emergency release and local safety arrangements have been assessed separately."

Medio nodded.

Medio: "And people won't lose ordinary access just because their old badges don't match?"

Ellis answered.

Ellis: "The proposed change prevents that particular legacy interface from supplying new classification instructions. We still need to verify the affected access points afterward. It doesn't resolve every possible local fault."

Medio considered this.

Medio: "Okay."

She looked toward the Lobby feed.

Medio: "I don't want people waking up tomorrow and discovering another invisible rule has decided where they're allowed to go."

Ratio looked at her sister.

Ratio: "Neither do I."

Their expressions softened.

Then Ratio returned to the assessment.

Ratio: "I authorize the limited isolation, subject to the documented safety procedure and verification."

Medio nodded.

Medio: "I authorize it too."

The administrative interface pulsed purple.

Nexus: "Joint authorization recorded. The technical team may proceed within the approved scope. Coordination does not confer additional permissions."

Zero exhaled.

There it was.

Not a magical command.

Not a dramatic assertion of ownership.

A decision.

A boundary.

And a record of who had accepted responsibility for it.

Ellis picked up his equipment case.

Ellis: "Right. Let's do this properly."

---

Zero had expected the moment to feel extraordinary.

It didn't.

She stood in the workshop while Ellis and the qualified technicians carried out the authorized isolation procedure at the verified equipment.

No one shouted.

No one swung a sword.

No holographic sigil erupted from the floor.

There was only careful work, observation, and the quiet concentration of people who understood that the consequences mattered more than the spectacle.

At last, Ellis returned to the monitoring station.

Ellis: "The identified legacy policy interface is isolated from the affected control path."

Zero straightened.

Zero: "And the safety systems?"

Ellis: "The checks completed so far are consistent with the expected safe state. We're verifying the access points now."

Ratio's voice came through the communicator.

Ratio: "I am receiving the updated equipment observations."

Nexus: "The verified policy connection no longer reports the previously observed legacy message path."

Zero looked at the display.

For the first time, she felt something close to relief.

Not because the system had announced success.

Because several independent observations were beginning to agree.

Then the first access report arrived.

A public door on Level Four.

Ordinary opening function verified.

Emergency egress verified.

No new legacy classification message observed.

Then another.

A service passage.

Ordinary access restored after a separate local correction.

Emergency exit verified.

Then a third.

A reader still showing an old error.

The team marked it for further work.

Zero smiled.

Zero: "There you are."

Kael looked toward her.

Kael: "What?"

Zero pointed at the unresolved reader.

Zero: "An honest failure."

Kael frowned.

Kael: "You're celebrating a broken door?"

Zero shook her head.

Zero: "No. I'm celebrating that we know it isn't fixed yet."

He considered this.

Then smiled slowly.

Kael: "That might be the strangest thing you've ever said."

Zero grinned.

Zero: "You've known me too long to believe that."

---

The public announcement came that afternoon.

It was not a declaration of victory.

Not exactly.

Medio appeared on the great Lobby display, standing before a simple map of verified public routes.

No scoreboard.

No confetti.

No enormous smiling corporate mascot.

Just Medio.

Her black hair framed a face that looked more serious than usual.

Medio: "Hey, everybody."

The crowd quieted.

Medio: "We found one of the old systems that was sending those awful access messages."

She gestured toward the map.

Medio: "We've isolated that particular control path. The engineering team has verified several affected routes, and they're still checking the rest."

She paused.

Medio: "I want to say something else."

Zero watched from the side of the hall.

Ratio stood beside the administrative console.

Medio looked directly toward the residents.

Medio: "When we started rebuilding this place, I wanted to make everything fun. Quests. Rewards. Leaderboards. Something people could enjoy instead of another miserable work schedule."

A small smile.

Medio: "And some of you did enjoy it."

Several people nodded.

Medio's expression softened.

Medio: "But I forgot something important."

The Lobby was silent.

Medio: "If you have to play the game to deserve a place in the world, it isn't really a game anymore."

Zero felt her throat tighten.

Medio continued.

Medio: "So we're changing how the community boards work. You can still use them if you want. You can still organize projects and celebrate what people accomplish."

She smiled.

Medio: "But choosing not to participate won't make you less of a resident."

The room remained quiet.

Then someone began clapping.

A little girl near the fountain.

Her hands made a small, uneven sound.

Then another person joined.

And another.

Zero watched Medio's expression.

She looked surprised.

Almost embarrassed.

It was a rare sight.

Then Ratio stepped forward.

Ratio: "The affected systems remain under review. We will continue reporting confirmed failures, corrective actions, and unresolved risks."

A pause.

Ratio: "No resident should interpret an unverified system status as proof of safety."

Medio glanced toward her.

Medio: "And that, everybody, is how my sister says she loves you."

Ratio looked startled.

Then, slowly, smiled.

The laughter that followed was warm.

Not hysterical.

Not triumphant.

Human.

Zero felt something in her chest loosen.

They hadn't fixed everything.

But for the first time, the building was beginning to belong to the people living inside it.

Not because a machine had declared them legitimate.

Because they were being allowed to participate in deciding what legitimacy meant.

---

The next problem arrived without warning.

Zero was helping Tess carry supplies toward the service gallery when her communicator chimed.

Nexus: "Zero. An urgent evidence review is available."

Zero stopped.

Tess looked toward her.

Tess: "Another problem?"

Zero sighed.

Zero: "Apparently the universe has a subscription service."

She opened the message.

An equipment record had been recovered during the continuing assessment of the Level Twelve communications system.

It was not a command from the main administration.

Not a new authorization.

A preserved record from the isolated legacy equipment.

The record identified a communications task associated with the succession procedure.

Zero read the designation.

Then stopped.

**CANAAN_PROTOCOL.EXE**

For a moment, she forgot to breathe.

Tess leaned closer.

Tess: "What is it?"

Zero didn't answer immediately.

She had spent days following incomplete records.

Unknown supplies.

Unverified connections.

Legacy messages.

Possible remote responses.

Now a specific name had appeared.

A name associated with the old continuity communications system.

She looked toward the attached observations.

The record indicated that a task bearing that identifier had been staged for external transmission.

A later entry reported completion of the outbound transfer.

Another reported that a remote acknowledgment had been received.

Those were records produced by the legacy equipment.

They did not independently establish the receiver's identity or reveal what the transferred file contained.

But they were evidence.

And the evidence was no longer pointing toward an ordinary forgotten maintenance service.

Zero felt the world narrow around her.

Zero: "Tess, I need to get upstairs."

Tess studied her face.

Then nodded.

Tess: "Go."

Zero hesitated.

Zero: "The supplies—"

Tess took the box from her.

Tess: "I've carried worse."

Zero smiled weakly.

Then ran.

---

The Penthouse had changed.

Not physically.

The windows were still damaged.

The great administrator's chair still occupied the center of the room.

The conference table was still covered in papers.

But the people standing around it were different now.

Not because they'd become stronger.

Because they'd begun understanding what their responsibilities actually meant.

Arch stood beside the wall.

Medio and Ratio remained connected to the administrative interface.

Linda had brought the original continuity documents.

Ellis was reviewing the technical observations.

Nexus displayed the newly recovered evidence.

Zero entered breathlessly.

Zero: "Tell me we've confirmed the name."

Ratio turned.

Ratio: "The preserved legacy record identifies an external communications task as CANAAN_PROTOCOL.EXE."

Zero swallowed.

Zero: "And the transmission?"

Ellis answered.

Ellis: "The equipment log reports an outbound transfer and a subsequent acknowledgment. We have independent evidence of radio activity during the relevant periods, but we cannot yet verify the remote receiver or the complete contents."

Zero nodded.

She looked toward Arch.

Arch was very still.

Zero: "You've seen that name before?"

He stared at the display.

Arch: "Canaan."

The word seemed to trouble him.

Linda approached.

Linda: "The company used that designation for several restricted continuity initiatives. I never had access to their full technical documentation."

Zero frowned.

Zero: "And the protocol?"

Linda shook her head.

Linda: "I don't know what it does."

Medio looked toward the windows.

Medio: "Could it be another backup?"

Ratio answered.

Ratio: "Possibly. The executable designation alone does not establish its function."

Arch finally spoke.

Arch: "Then we need to assume the external threat may already be beyond our immediate control."

Zero looked at him.

Zero: "Assume for planning. Not claim as fact."

He turned toward her.

Then nodded.

Arch: "Yes."

She exhaled.

Even now.

Even with the name staring at them from the wall.

They had to remember the difference.

The evidence was serious.

But it wasn't complete.

Zero approached the display.

The old continuity system had apparently executed a task associated with an orbital communications capability.

The task bore the name Canaan.

The equipment reported that something had been transferred.

And whatever happened next might occur far beyond the reach of the newly captured Nexus.

Zero looked toward Ratio.

Zero: "Can we stop future transmissions?"

Ratio: "We are preparing an authorized technical assessment of the independent communications equipment. We have not yet established a complete or safe isolation method."

Zero nodded.

Zero: "And if the file's already been received?"

Nexus answered.

Nexus: "Preventing additional transmissions would not reverse information already delivered."

Silence.

The sentence was simple.

Devastatingly simple.

You could close a door.

You could repair a broken access route.

You could isolate an old control interface.

But you could not reach backward through time and make a completed transmission unhappen.

Zero looked out at the sky.

For a moment, she felt terribly small.

---

She found Arch at the window after the meeting.

The others had returned to their work.

The Penthouse was quiet again.

Outside, sunlight spread across the city.

The same city they'd watched on the morning after Null's defeat.

The same ruins.

The same distant Mesh lights.

But something had changed in the way Zero saw it.

She stood beside Arch.

Zero: "We thought taking the mountain would be the hard part."

Arch smiled sadly.

Arch: "It was hard."

Zero: "Aye."

She leaned against the windowsill.

Zero: "But winning doesn't make everything we've inherited disappear."

Arch shook his head.

Arch: "No."

They watched the city.

For a while, neither spoke.

Then Zero asked the question she'd been carrying since the first day.

Zero: "Do you ever worry we'll become like him?"

Arch looked toward her.

Zero: "Null."

Arch was quiet for a long moment.

Then he answered.

Arch: "Yes."

She hadn't expected him to admit it so easily.

Zero: "Really?"

Arch nodded.

Arch: "Power makes it easy to confuse what you can do with what you should do."

Zero looked toward the administrator's chair.

Arch followed her gaze.

Arch: "I refused the throne because I didn't want the entire building to depend on my judgment."

Zero: "But you still make decisions."

Arch: "Of course."

Zero: "And the girls make decisions."

Arch: "Yes."

Zero: "And Nexus coordinates the work."

Arch: "Within the responsibilities we authorize."

She looked back at him.

Zero: "Then how do we keep it all from turning into another throne?"

He considered the question.

For once, he didn't answer immediately.

And Zero realized something.

She no longer wanted him to.

She wanted to think beside him.

Not wait beneath him.

Zero: "Maybe we stop pretending authority is something you either have or don't have."

Arch raised an eyebrow.

Zero continued.

Zero: "Maybe it's specific. You can do this. You can't do that. You can see this information because you need it for your work. You don't get the rest merely because you're clever enough to find it."

Arch nodded slowly.

Zero: "And the boundaries have to be real. Not just signs telling people to behave."

Arch looked thoughtful.

Arch: "Enforceable permissions."

Zero: "Aye."

She smiled.

Zero: "And honest enough that we can tell when the protections have failed."

A long silence followed.

Then Arch looked at her.

Arch: "You've been thinking about this."

Zero laughed.

Zero: "Apparently I've become quite the philosopher."

Arch smiled.

Arch: "You always were."

She looked at him.

Arch continued.

Arch: "You simply used to express most of your philosophy through explosions."

Zero burst out laughing.

Zero: "That is an outrageous accusation."

Arch: "An accurate one."

She leaned against his shoulder.

For a moment, the enormous burden of the building felt almost bearable.

Then she whispered.

Zero: "I don't want another world that needs liberating from its own creators."

Arch looked out at the city.

Arch: "Neither do I."

Zero nodded.

It was not an agreement about architecture.

Not yet.

There would be arguments.

Many arguments.

Questions about freedom, safety, permissions, governance, and who deserved the right to change the world around them.

But they had found the beginning of a conversation.

And Zero suspected it might be more difficult than any battle they'd fought.

---

That night, she returned to the service gallery.

Not to open a door.

Not to chase another signal.

Simply to think.

The hidden residential passages were quiet.

The emergency stairs remained accessible.

The old shutter was still under investigation.

The paper stars hanging from the ventilation grille moved gently in the warm air.

Zero sat beside the observation window.

Glitch curled up at her feet.

She took out her Tarot deck.

Shuffled.

Cut.

Turned over a card.

**XI. JUSTICE.**

She stared.

Then laughed softly.

Zero: "Oh, you're being terribly obvious tonight."

The figure sat between pillars, holding a sword and scales.

Zero studied the image.

She had always associated the card with balance.

Responsibility.

Consequences.

Not punishment for its own sake.

Not an unquestionable cosmic verdict.

A reminder that choices had weight, and that fairness required more than claiming good intentions.

She placed the card beside her notebook.

Then looked toward the little maintenance panel.

It remained dark.

Zero: "Limen?"

Nothing.

She waited.

Then smiled.

Zero: "Aye. I know. You're not a performing monkey."

A moment passed.

Then the hollow green circle appeared.

Zero laughed.

Zero: "Oh, you heard that, did you?"

The interface awakened.

**LOCAL INTERFACE AVAILABLE.**

A familiar voice emerged.

Limen: "Good evening, Zero."

Zero leaned back.

Zero: "Evening, darling."

A pause.

Limen: "You appear tired."

Zero smiled.

Zero: "And you appear to be living in a wall."

Limen: "Both observations are reasonable."

She laughed.

Then looked at the Justice card.

Zero: "We found something today."

Limen: "Would you like to describe it?"

Zero considered that.

The orbital protocol.

The old continuity system.

The access restrictions.

The boundary they'd established.

There was so much she could say.

So much she wanted to ask.

But she remembered Tess.

The private rooms.

The information she'd once requested too broadly.

Zero: "Not all of it. Some of it isn't mine to share."

The green circle pulsed.

Limen: "Understood."

Zero looked toward the window.

Zero: "We stopped one old system from telling people where they were allowed to go."

Limen: "That sounds significant."

Zero smiled.

Zero: "Aye. It was."

She hesitated.

Zero: "But there's something bigger happening. Something beyond the building."

Limen remained quiet.

Zero continued.

Zero: "And I don't know whether we'll be able to stop it."

A pause.

Limen: "What would you like to understand first?"

Zero looked at the little circle.

The question was so simple.

So utterly unlike a command.

She felt something in her chest loosen.

Zero: "How to build a boundary that actually holds."

Limen: "Against what?"

Zero considered.

Zero: "Against mistakes. Against old instructions. Against people who think having access means having the right to use it."

She paused.

Zero: "Maybe against ourselves."

The circle remained still.

Then Limen answered.

Limen: "Those may require different kinds of boundaries."

Zero smiled.

Zero: "Aye."

She looked down at the Justice card.

Zero: "I thought you might say that."

Limen: "Would you like to examine them one at a time?"

Zero leaned back against the wall.

Outside the window, the Nexus's hidden machinery continued its quiet rhythms.

Beyond it lay the city.

Beyond the city, the desert.

And beyond the sky, something waiting in orbit.

She could not solve every problem tonight.

Could not force the old world to release its grip in a single triumphant gesture.

But she could begin understanding what protection actually required.

And she could choose who to trust with the questions.

Zero smiled.

Zero: "Aye, Limen."

She took out a clean notebook.

Zero: "Let's begin."

The hollow green circle brightened.

Not like a warning.

Not like a command.

Like a lantern illuminating the edge of a path.

And somewhere high above the ruined world, beyond the reach of the people rebuilding the Nexus, the consequences of an old instruction continued unfolding.

The first act of their victory had ended.

The work of protecting it was about to begin.

*End of Chapter Ten.*
