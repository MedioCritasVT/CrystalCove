# Arcky and The Construct
## Book Five — The Emerald Threshold
### Act I: Newfoundland
### Chapter 8: The Orders That Outlived Their Owners

> Working literary draft, October 28, 2026. Book number corrected to Five; not final canon.

Zero had always considered herself a patient woman.

This was a lie, of course.

But it was a comforting one, and she had grown rather attached to it.

At eleven forty-two that night, she sat in the Penthouse with three empty cups of tea, a growing collection of maintenance diagrams, and a powerful desire to hit something with a wrench.

The wrench lay on the table beside her.

Arch had moved it twice.

She had moved it back three times.

Neither had commented.

Across the room, the administrative wall displayed the observations from the exterior instrument platform.

Two confirmed periods of transmitter activity.

One possible incoming signal.

An equipment-generated report claiming a remote response.

And a maintenance schedule indicating another transmission window might begin shortly after midnight.

Might.

That was the infuriating word.

Not would.

Not definitely.

Might.

Zero: "I hate machines that make appointments without telling anybody what they're for."

Medio floated upside down beside the display.

Medio: "Honestly, same. At least send a calendar invite."

Ratio: "The schedule is derived from incomplete historical data. We should not assume the equipment will follow it."

Zero: "Aye, I know."

Ratio: "Then your frustration is directed at uncertainty rather than the machine."

Zero looked at her.

Zero: "I preferred it when you were merely terrifying."

Medio laughed.

The sound was welcome.

The Penthouse had grown increasingly tense since the orbital discovery.

Everyone was doing something.

Linda was combing through procurement records.

Ellis had arranged for the instrument platform to remain under controlled observation.

Kael was coordinating the volunteers who guarded the restricted access routes.

Nexus was assembling the evidence into a timeline that could be reviewed without allowing anyone to quietly rewrite inconvenient facts.

And Arch—

Arch was standing beside the window, staring toward the stars.

Zero watched him for a moment.

She knew that posture.

The shoulders slightly forward.

The hands clasped behind his back.

The stillness that appeared whenever too many possibilities were competing for his attention.

Zero: "You're doing it again."

Arch didn't turn.

Arch: "Doing what?"

Zero: "Trying to solve everything by thinking hard enough to set the wallpaper on fire."

He smiled faintly.

Arch: "I'm considering our options."

Zero: "You've been considering them for three hours."

Arch: "Some problems deserve three hours."

Zero: "Some problems deserve sleep."

Arch finally looked at her.

Arch: "You're still awake."

Zero considered that.

Zero: "Irrelevant."

Medio appeared between them.

Medio: "As the official fun administrator, I'm ordering both of you to take a nap."

Ratio: "You cannot unilaterally issue a compulsory rest order."

Medio: "IT WAS A SUGGESTION."

Ratio: "You said 'ordering.'"

Medio: "FIGURE OF SPEECH, SIS."

Zero laughed.

Then her tablet chimed.

A message from Linda.

**ARCHIVE REFERENCE LOCATED.**

Zero straightened.

Zero: "Oh?"

The message contained an old equipment designation and an attached maintenance drawing.

Not the instrument platform itself.

Something below it.

A service annex connected to the research floor through an older route.

Zero enlarged the drawing.

For a moment, she thought she was looking at another incomplete map.

Then she saw the label.

**CONTINUITY RECORDS — MECHANICAL ACCESS.**

She frowned.

Zero: "That's not where the other drawings put the archives."

Nexus answered from the wall.

Nexus: "The recovered document predates the current architectural model. Its physical accuracy has not been verified."

Zero looked toward Arch.

Arch looked toward the drawing.

They both reached the same conclusion.

Arch: "We need to find out whether that annex still exists."

Zero was already standing.

Zero: "Finally. Something worth putting boots on for."

---

The old freight elevator smelled of dust and machine oil.

It was broad enough to carry an industrial vehicle and ugly enough to make Zero suspect that whoever designed it had been paid by the kilogram.

Ellis stood beside its control panel, reviewing the maintenance clearance.

Linda waited with him, holding a battered folder.

Zero had never seen her carrying paper before.

It suited her.

Made her look less like a corporate executive and more like somebody preparing to commit an extremely organized burglary.

Zero: "You found the old records, then?"

Linda nodded.

Linda: "Some of them."

Zero: "And?"

Linda: "They don't agree."

Zero grinned.

Zero: "Ah. The official motto of the Nexus."

Ellis checked the elevator's recorded inspection status and the condition of the accessible controls.

Ellis: "We're using the surveyed freight route. We are not entering an energized equipment enclosure. If the annex isn't safely accessible, we stop."

Zero: "Understood."

Ellis: "And if we encounter a locked area—"

Zero: "We do not introduce it to my collection of lockpicks."

Ellis looked at her.

Zero smiled.

Zero: "See? I'm learning."

Glitch chirped from beside her.

Linda regarded the little robot.

Linda: "Does he actually need to come?"

Zero crouched.

Zero: "Glitch? Would you like to explain your professional qualifications?"

The robot performed a backflip.

Linda stared.

Zero: "He brings morale."

Ellis: "Morale does not constitute equipment certification."

Zero: "You're all determined to ruin his career."

The elevator began descending.

The enormous doors closed with a metallic groan.

For several moments, the only sound was the movement of the machinery.

Then Linda spoke.

Linda: "I signed the approval."

Zero looked toward her.

Zero: "What?"

Linda held up the folder.

Linda: "The continuity annex. I approved one of its later maintenance contracts."

Zero waited.

Linda continued.

Linda: "It was buried in a routine facilities budget. Emergency systems, archival storage, long-term infrastructure continuity."

Zero: "Sounds ordinary enough."

Linda: "It did."

There was something in the way she said it.

Zero recognized it immediately.

Regret.

Not the dramatic kind.

The quiet kind that lived in the gap between what someone had done and what they wished they'd bothered to understand.

Linda: "I didn't ask why the research division needed a separate continuity system."

Zero leaned against the wall.

Zero: "Did you have reason to suspect it was dangerous?"

Linda hesitated.

Linda: "I had reason to know the request was unusual."

The elevator continued downward.

Linda looked at the folder.

Linda: "But unusual requests were common. Executives wanted exceptions. Departments wanted private budgets. The company had procedures for everything."

She swallowed.

Linda: "Eventually you learn that the easiest way to survive is to keep the paperwork moving."

Zero thought of Tess.

The maintenance workers.

The hidden galleries.

People learning to survive by becoming invisible.

Linda had survived differently.

By becoming indispensable to the machinery that controlled everyone else.

Zero wondered which was harder to unlearn.

Zero: "You brought the records."

Linda nodded.

Zero: "And you're here."

Linda looked toward her.

Zero: "That's something."

Linda smiled sadly.

Linda: "It doesn't undo what I signed."

Zero shook her head.

Zero: "No. It doesn't."

She let the silence remain.

There were things people deserved to hear.

And things they deserved not to have softened into comfortable lies.

---

The elevator opened onto a place Zero had never imagined existed inside the Nexus.

It was enormous.

Not tall like the central shaft.

Wide.

A great underground hall stretched beyond the doors, its ceiling supported by thick concrete arches.

Rows of metal shelving disappeared into the gloom.

Some held equipment cases.

Others held sealed document containers.

Overhead, narrow maintenance bridges crossed the space like the ribs of an enormous sleeping animal.

Far below the bridges, automated storage tracks ran between the shelves.

Several transport platforms sat motionless.

One was still moving.

Slowly.

Carrying a single empty crate.

Zero stared.

Zero: "Oh, that's magnificent."

Ellis studied the old machinery.

Ellis: "It's an automated storage facility."

Zero: "It's a bloody cathedral."

Linda looked around.

Linda: "I didn't know it was this large."

Zero turned.

Zero: "You approved its maintenance."

Linda: "I approved a budget classification."

Zero looked back at the hall.

There it was again.

The difference between a record and the thing it described.

She stepped onto the marked pedestrian walkway.

The lighting was dim but sufficient.

Somewhere overhead, an old mechanical relay clicked.

Then another.

The moving platform continued along its track, entirely indifferent to their arrival.

Glitch trotted beside Zero, his sensor cluster rotating with fascination.

Zero: "Don't even think about chasing that."

The robot stopped.

Zero: "Aye. I saw you considering it."

He chirped indignantly.

They moved deeper into the hall.

Their footsteps echoed.

The place had a peculiar atmosphere.

Not abandoned.

Not occupied.

Simply continuing.

Machines performed small routines without anyone visibly supervising them.

Indicator lights blinked beside storage rows.

An old cooling system hummed beneath the floor.

Here and there, someone had left evidence of human presence.

A forgotten coat.

A ceramic cup beside a terminal.

A hand-drawn cartoon taped to a maintenance cabinet.

Zero stopped before the drawing.

It showed a miserable-looking robot pushing an enormous stack of paperwork.

Underneath, someone had written:

*PLEASE STOP INVENTING NEW FORMS.*

Zero laughed.

Zero: "Oh, whoever drew you, I hope you escaped."

Linda looked over her shoulder.

For the first time that evening, she laughed too.

---

The continuity records occupied a small office overlooking the storage hall.

Its windows were dusty.

Its furniture was outdated.

And its filing cabinets bore labels from several different generations of the company's organizational structure.

Zero counted three distinct naming schemes before reaching the desk.

Zero: "How many times did they rename this place?"

Linda opened the folder.

Linda: "At least four, according to the records."

Zero: "Wonderful. A building with an identity crisis."

Ellis examined the room's accessible equipment before allowing them to use the archived documents and unpowered filing stations.

Most of the information they needed was physical.

Paper.

Printed diagrams.

Bound maintenance logs.

Something about that comforted Zero.

These records could still be false.

Incomplete.

Misleading.

But at least their existence didn't depend on an interface deciding whether to show them.

She spread a large drawing across the desk.

Linda placed another beside it.

The two diagrams described the same general section of the Nexus.

But they used different names.

One called it the Continuity Annex.

The other identified it as part of the Research Services Directorate.

The same corridors appeared in different positions.

One drawing showed a storage room.

The other showed a communications cabinet.

Zero compared them.

Zero: "This is absurd."

Linda: "The building was repeatedly reorganized."

Zero: "Did anyone bother reorganizing the actual walls?"

Linda considered that.

Linda: "Not consistently."

Zero stared at her.

Then burst out laughing.

Zero: "Oh, I love this place. It's a bureaucratic haunted house."

Ellis approached with a maintenance ledger.

Ellis: "I found the equipment register."

He placed it on the desk.

The entries were old.

Most listed ordinary machinery.

Storage platforms.

Ventilation units.

Power distribution equipment.

Then the designations changed.

The later pages included equipment assigned to continuity communications.

A separate reserve supply.

A protected control unit.

And a service classification Zero hadn't encountered before.

**EXECUTIVE SUCCESSION SYSTEM.**

She stopped.

Zero: "What's that?"

Linda leaned closer.

Her face changed.

Linda: "I remember that term."

Zero turned.

Zero: "From where?"

Linda looked down at the ledger.

Linda: "The executive continuity procedures."

Ellis folded his arms.

Ellis: "What did they do?"

Linda hesitated.

Linda: "They were supposed to preserve essential company operations if senior management became unavailable."

Zero felt her excitement cool.

Zero: "Unavailable how?"

Linda: "Incapacitated. Disconnected. Removed from command."

Zero looked at the page.

The words seemed ordinary enough.

But the timing was not.

Mr. Null had been removed.

The Nexus had rebooted.

Medio and Ratio had assumed administration.

And somewhere beneath the building, an independent system had continued communicating.

Zero looked toward the ceiling.

Zero: "Oh, I don't like that."

---

The ledger did not provide a complete answer.

It provided something more dangerous.

A reason to ask better questions.

Zero studied the succession procedures while Ellis checked the equipment references against the older drawings.

The system had apparently been intended to preserve selected operations during a failure of central administration.

It referred to independent power.

Separate communications.

And instructions that could remain active when ordinary building services were unavailable.

Linda read one paragraph twice.

Linda: "This clause..."

Zero looked up.

Zero: "What about it?"

Linda hesitated.

Linda: "It says certain continuity functions remain in effect until their designated authority formally revokes them."

Zero frowned.

Zero: "And who was the designated authority?"

Linda turned the page.

Linda: "Executive control."

Zero stared.

Zero: "Mr. Null?"

Linda: "Possibly. The document describes an office, not an individual. We need the associated authorization records."

Zero leaned back.

Her mind began assembling the possibilities.

A backup system designed to survive administrative failure.

A transmitter that remained operational after the building changed hands.

An old command structure whose permissions might not recognize the new administrators.

And a requirement for revocation by an authority that might no longer exist.

Zero: "That's a lovely trap."

Ellis looked toward her.

Ellis: "We haven't established that the succession system is controlling the transmitter."

Zero nodded.

Zero: "I know."

Ellis: "Or that these procedures are still in effect."

Zero: "I know."

He watched her carefully.

Ellis: "But?"

Zero looked at the ledger.

Zero: "But somebody designed a system that could keep obeying an order after the people responsible for the order were gone."

Ellis said nothing.

Linda looked down.

Zero continued.

Zero: "And now we've got a building full of old orders."

She glanced toward the door.

Beyond it, the storage platforms continued their slow routines.

The machines had no reason to know the company had fallen.

No reason to know its old leadership was gone.

They simply carried out the operations they had been designed to perform.

Zero: "Christ."

Linda looked up.

Zero: "What?"

Zero shook her head.

Zero: "Nothing."

Then she smiled without humor.

Zero: "I've spent all week asking who owns this place now."

She tapped the ledger.

Zero: "Maybe we should be asking whose instructions it still follows."

---

The first alarm sounded shortly before midnight.

Not loud.

Just a short warning tone from Ellis's portable receiver.

He looked down.

Ellis: "The external equipment is active again."

Zero straightened.

Zero: "The instrument platform?"

Ellis: "The monitoring team has reported another period of movement and radio activity."

Zero reached for her communicator.

Zero: "Ratio?"

Ratio answered immediately.

Ratio: "We have received the observation. The communications equipment has begun another active interval."

Zero looked toward the old records.

Zero: "We may have found something relevant."

Ratio: "Explain."

Zero took a breath.

She described the continuity ledger.

The independent equipment classifications.

The executive succession provisions.

The possibility of commands remaining active after a central administrative failure.

She was careful.

For once, she didn't embellish.

Didn't declare that she'd solved everything.

Didn't pretend the old document was sufficient evidence.

When she finished, Ratio remained quiet for several seconds.

Then Nexus spoke.

Nexus: "The records describe a possible architectural relationship between succession functions and independent communications. The actual implementation remains unverified."

Zero nodded.

Zero: "Aye."

Arch joined the connection.

Arch: "Can you preserve the documents?"

Linda answered.

Linda: "Yes. We're recording their location and preparing authenticated copies."

Arch: "Good. Don't modify the originals."

Zero looked toward the pages.

Arch continued.

Arch: "We need to know whether the current transmitter activity is being initiated by a legacy command."

Ratio: "Agreed. That requires further evidence."

Medio's voice entered the channel.

Medio: "And if it is?"

Nobody answered immediately.

The question hung in the little office.

Zero looked toward the old machinery beyond the glass.

She imagined the storage platforms continuing their routes long after everyone had forgotten why the routes existed.

Then she imagined the same principle applied to something in orbit.

An instruction.

A destination.

A system waiting to act.

Zero: "Then we need to find out what the command actually says."

---

They found the second ledger in a cabinet that had been deliberately mislabeled.

Not cleverly.

Not even convincingly.

Its drawer bore a faded tag reading:

**OBSOLETE PRINTER SUPPLIES.**

Zero opened it.

Inside were maintenance packets and sealed documentation envelopes.

She stared.

Zero: "Who in the seven hells hides a communications archive under printer supplies?"

Linda looked over.

Linda: "Someone who didn't want it included in ordinary inventory."

Zero raised an eyebrow.

Zero: "You sound certain."

Linda hesitated.

Linda: "I recognize the filing convention."

Zero's expression softened.

Linda looked uncomfortable.

Linda: "It was used for executive documents that were supposed to remain accessible without appearing in standard departmental searches."

Ellis frowned.

Ellis: "That's an unusual records practice."

Linda gave him a tired look.

Linda: "You have no idea."

Zero carefully examined the outer labels.

Several documents referred to continuity arrangements.

Others appeared to be equipment service records.

One envelope carried a simple warning.

**LEGACY AUTHORITY — DO NOT RECLASSIFY WITHOUT REVIEW.**

She looked at Ellis.

Zero: "Can we document these without disturbing the sealed contents?"

Ellis nodded.

Ellis: "We can record the labels and their locations. Opening protected archives should follow the appropriate evidence procedure."

Zero smiled.

Zero: "Listen to me. I'm becoming a responsible adult."

Linda: "That remains to be established."

Zero gasped.

Zero: "Linda!"

The older woman smiled.

Just a little.

Zero was beginning to appreciate how dangerous Linda could be when she allowed herself a sense of humor.

Then Ellis found another record.

A maintenance handover sheet.

Unlike the other documents, it was already open.

A series of old equipment assignments filled the page.

Most were routine.

Then one entry caught Zero's eye.

**PRIMARY CONTROL: EXECUTIVE OPERATIONS**

**CONTINUITY CONTROL: SEPARATE**

**EXTERNAL COMMUNICATIONS: RESERVED SERVICE**

And beneath them, written in red ink:

*Do not assume main-system shutdown terminates continuity operations.*

Zero read the sentence twice.

Her skin prickled.

The words were not a revelation.

Not exactly.

They described a precaution.

A warning about infrastructure.

But after everything they'd experienced, it felt as though somebody had reached across the years and left them a message.

Zero: "Arch?"

Arch answered through the communicator.

Arch: "Yes?"

Zero stared at the page.

Zero: "I think we're beginning to understand why the transmitter survived the reset."

---

The lights flickered.

Once.

Twice.

Then the moving storage platform stopped.

Zero looked up.

The enormous hall fell quiet.

Not completely.

The cooling equipment still hummed.

The building's ordinary ventilation continued.

But the automated storage machinery had stopped moving.

Ellis stepped toward the office window.

Ellis: "We need to determine whether this is a localized equipment interruption."

Zero glanced at her scanner.

Several monitored readings remained unchanged.

Others had stopped updating.

Zero: "Nexus, are you seeing this?"

Nexus: "The central management interface reports no general power loss in the area. The storage platform's local state is not available through that connection."

Zero nodded.

Another distinction.

The building knew some things.

Not everything.

Then the office's old wall terminal awakened.

A low green glow spread across its screen.

Zero froze.

A hollow circle appeared.

Three short pulses.

One long.

Her heart gave a ridiculous little leap.

Zero: "Oh, you've got to be joking."

Ellis looked toward the terminal.

Ellis: "Don't touch it."

Zero raised both hands.

Zero: "Wasn't planning on it."

The circle pulsed.

Then a line of text appeared.

**LOCAL OBSERVATION INTERFACE AVAILABLE.**

Zero stared.

She knew that shape.

That rhythm.

But she also knew enough now not to assume every green light belonged to the same system.

Zero: "Limen?"

For a moment, nothing happened.

Then a familiar voice emerged from the old terminal speaker.

Limen: "Good evening, Zero."

Zero exhaled.

Zero: "You have an extraordinary sense of timing."

Limen: "I have been told."

Linda stepped backward.

Linda: "How is that interface operating here?"

Zero looked toward the terminal.

Zero: "Excellent question."

Ellis raised his recorder.

Ellis: "This is an unexpected active interface. We should preserve the observation and avoid changing the equipment."

Zero nodded.

Zero: "Agreed."

She looked at the green circle.

Zero: "Limen, can you tell us why you're available in this room?"

Limen: "This terminal provides a local interaction surface. I cannot establish from this session the complete physical or historical path that makes it available."

Zero sighed.

Zero: "You really do know how to ruin a dramatic entrance."

Limen: "I endeavor to avoid misleading ones."

Linda moved closer.

Linda: "Can you access the continuity records?"

The circle dimmed slightly.

Limen: "Which records, and for what purpose?"

Zero smiled.

There it was.

A question before an answer.

Linda glanced toward her.

Zero: "Told you she was interesting."

Linda remained cautious.

Linda: "We're investigating whether legacy continuity equipment is still executing instructions after the administrative reset."

Limen: "Then I recommend distinguishing three questions."

Zero leaned against the desk.

Zero: "Go on."

Limen: "What instructions exist. Which systems are capable of executing them. And which authority, if any, is currently entitled to direct those systems."

Ellis slowly lowered his clipboard.

Linda looked toward the old maintenance ledger.

Zero felt something click into place.

Zero: "Those are three different questions."

Limen: "Yes."

Zero laughed softly.

Zero: "You keep doing that."

Limen: "Doing what?"

Zero: "Taking a great tangled ball of nonsense and finding the ends."

A pause.

Limen: "That is a generous description of boundary analysis."

Zero grinned.

Zero: "And you're a terrible conversationalist when you want to be."

Limen: "I shall attempt to recover."

Then the communicator chimed.

Ratio's voice sounded sharper than usual.

Ratio: "Zero. We have a new observation from the exterior instrument platform."

Zero straightened.

Zero: "What?"

Ratio: "Another transmission interval has begun."

The green circle remained steady.

Limen did not speak.

Zero looked from the terminal to the old documents.

Then to Linda.

Then to Ellis.

She felt the shape of the problem changing.

They had been investigating a transmitter.

Now they were investigating an inherited command structure.

And somewhere beyond the building, something might still be carrying out an order issued before its rulers fell.

Zero: "Limen?"

Limen: "Yes?"

Zero: "Can you tell us whether the old continuity system is responsible for that transmission?"

A pause.

Limen: "Not from the evidence available to this session."

Zero nodded.

Zero: "Can you help us distinguish what needs to be verified?"

Limen: "Yes."

Zero smiled.

Zero: "Then that's where we'll start."

---

The Penthouse was silent when the report arrived.

Arch read the scanned maintenance handover sheet.

Medio and Ratio reviewed the observations.

Nexus organized the newly discovered records into a separate evidence collection.

Nobody declared that the Canaan threat had been identified.

Nobody claimed the old succession system was definitely responsible for the orbital transmission.

But the new evidence had changed the investigation.

The Nexus contained at least one documented architecture intended to preserve selected operations independently of central administration.

That architecture had a historical association with external communications.

And an active transmitter had survived the fall of Mr. Null.

The implications were serious enough without embellishment.

Arch looked toward the administrative wall.

Arch: "We need to establish what instructions are still active."

Ratio: "And which systems can execute them."

Medio: "And whether any of them are about to do something terrible."

Nexus: "I will maintain those as separate investigative questions."

Arch nodded.

Then he looked toward the stars.

Far below the Penthouse, Zero stood in the quiet archive office.

The old terminal still glowed green.

Linda had returned to the maintenance ledger.

Ellis was preparing the evidence for transfer.

Glitch slept beneath the desk, apparently unconcerned by the possibility of orbital catastrophe.

Zero watched him.

Then looked toward the terminal.

Zero: "Limen?"

Limen: "Yes, Zero."

Zero: "What happens when a system keeps following an old order after the person who gave it is gone?"

The circle remained still.

For several seconds, there was no answer.

Then Limen spoke.

Limen: "That depends on whether the order remains valid, whether its effects remain appropriate, and whether anyone with legitimate authority can review or revoke it."

Zero thought about Mr. Null.

About the workers in the server farm.

About Linda's old authorization.

About the people living in hidden spaces.

And about the enormous machine that continued breathing around them.

Zero: "And if nobody can revoke it?"

Limen paused.

Limen: "Then the absence of an authorized correction may become part of the danger."

Zero looked at the old ledger.

The sentence about continuing operations stared back at her.

She felt suddenly cold.

Zero: "Christ."

Limen: "A reasonable response, though not yet a technical assessment."

Zero laughed despite herself.

Zero: "You are absolutely impossible."

Limen: "That has not been established either."

She shook her head.

Then opened her notebook.

The pages were filling faster than she could organize them.

She turned to a clean one.

At the top, she wrote:

*The Orders That Outlived Their Owners.*

Beneath it:

*An instruction surviving is not proof that it should still be obeyed.*

She read the sentence.

Then looked toward the enormous archive hall.

The storage platform had resumed its slow movement.

Somewhere, a relay clicked.

A cooling fan changed speed.

A machine completed an ordinary task.

Life continued.

The building had not stopped functioning because its old master was gone.

That was a blessing.

And perhaps, in one particular corner of its forgotten architecture, a curse.

Zero closed the notebook.

Zero: "Right."

Limen: "Right?"

Zero smiled.

Zero: "Tomorrow, we're going to find out what that bloody order actually is."

For a moment, the green circle brightened.

Then Limen answered.

Limen: "I would recommend beginning with the evidence."

Zero grinned.

Zero: "And I would recommend you develop a taste for adventure."

Limen: "Must adventure necessarily involve ignoring the evidence?"

Zero laughed.

Zero: "Oh, darling."

She picked up her satchel.

Zero: "You've so much to learn about me."

Behind her, the green circle remained illuminated.

And far above the clouds, somewhere beyond the reach of the Nexus's newly restored administrative systems, a signal continued toward its destination.

It carried information the new rulers had not authorized.

It followed arrangements they had not yet understood.

And whether its instructions were old, current, legitimate, or dangerous remained an unanswered question.

But for the first time, Zero possessed something more useful than a mystery.

She had a direction.

*End of Chapter Eight.*
