# Arcky and The Construct
## Book Five — The Emerald Threshold
### Act I: Newfoundland
### Chapter 9: The Bell Without a Hand

> Working literary draft from October 28, 2026. Book number corrected to Five; not final canon.

The first sign of trouble was a printer.

Not an alarm.

Not an explosion.

Not a terrible message from the heavens.

A printer.

It sat on a little desk inside the Continuity Annex, beneath a faded notice reminding employees to conserve paper.

At precisely three minutes past midnight, it began producing documents.

Zero watched the first page slide into its tray.

Then the second.

Then the third.

Zero: "Oh, you've got to be kidding."

Linda approached.

Linda: "I didn't submit a print request."

Ellis looked up from the inspection records.

Ellis: "Neither did I."

The printer continued.

Clack.

Whirr.

Clack.

Zero reached for the first page, then stopped.

She glanced toward Ellis.

Zero: "Am I allowed to touch the haunted paperwork?"

Ellis examined the machine from a safe distance.

Ellis: "The output tray is accessible. Document the condition and preserve the pages in order."

Zero smiled.

Zero: "You really are a romantic."

She photographed the output and carefully lifted the first sheet.

Across the top, in uncompromising corporate typography, were four words.

**EXECUTIVE CONTINUITY PROCEDURE**

Below them:

**SUCCESSION EVENT DETECTED.**

**PRIOR ADMINISTRATIVE AUTHORITY: UNAVAILABLE.**

**RECONCILIATION SEQUENCE: INITIATED.**

Zero read the final line twice.

Zero: "Linda?"

Linda's face had gone pale.

Zero: "I don't like your expression."

Linda took the page.

For several seconds, she simply stared.

Then she sat down.

Linda: "Oh."

It was a very small word.

Coming from Linda, Zero found it considerably more frightening than a scream.

Zero: "Talk to me."

Linda rubbed her forehead.

Linda: "Reconciliation was an executive continuity procedure."

Zero: "Reconciliation of what?"

Linda looked up.

Linda: "Everything."

---

The old procedure had been described in reassuring language.

*Restore operational consistency.*

*Verify authorized personnel.*

*Reestablish controlled access.*

*Preserve business-critical continuity.*

Zero hated every word.

Not because the concepts were inherently unreasonable.

Because she'd learned that the distance between a reasonable phrase and a terrible implementation could contain an entire civilization's suffering.

Linda spread the pages across the desk.

Linda: "When the executive control system failed, certain continuity services were supposed to reconcile the building against a stored organizational baseline."

Zero: "An old snapshot?"

Linda: "Something like that. Approved access classifications, personnel records, equipment assignments, service priorities."

Zero frowned.

Zero: "And what happens if the building's changed since the snapshot?"

Linda didn't answer immediately.

Zero leaned closer.

Zero: "Linda."

Linda: "The procedure was intended to resolve discrepancies."

Zero: "How?"

Linda examined the next page.

Linda: "By restoring established classifications or forwarding exceptions for executive review."

Zero stared.

Then laughed.

It was not a happy laugh.

Zero: "Oh, that's magnificent."

Ellis looked toward her.

Zero: "We've taken the building, the entire administration has changed, half the residents aren't on the old records, and somewhere there's a machine trying to put everything back the way it was."

Linda's expression tightened.

Linda: "We don't know whether it can enforce those instructions."

Zero nodded.

She was getting better at that.

At not allowing an excellent theory to outrun the evidence.

Zero: "Aye. We don't."

She looked toward the printer.

It was still working.

Another sheet emerged.

**RECONCILIATION PHASE ONE: RECORD COMPARISON**

**RECONCILIATION PHASE TWO: ACCESS ALIGNMENT**

**RECONCILIATION PHASE THREE: EXCEPTION ESCALATION**

Zero picked it up.

Zero: "That's not an especially comforting sequence."

Ellis examined the page.

Ellis: "We need to determine whether this is a newly generated instruction, an old scheduled report, or a local response to another system."

Zero looked at him.

Zero: "You're going to make me become a proper engineer against my will."

Ellis: "I thought you already were one."

She grinned.

Zero: "You're finally learning to flirt."

Then her communicator sounded.

Medio's voice filled the little office.

Medio: "Zero?"

Something in her tone made Zero straighten.

Medio wasn't joking.

Zero: "What's happened?"

Medio: "The Quest Boards are changing."

---

The Level 4 gym had become one of the busiest places in the Nexus.

It was no longer simply a battleground between gamers and athletes.

People gathered there to organize work, compare skills, request supplies, and occasionally argue about the appropriate music for moving furniture.

Medio had transformed the enormous central display into a colorful community board.

Some residents loved it.

Others ignored it.

And a few had begun asking whether they could have a noticeboard that didn't involve experience points.

Medio had been considering that request.

Now, without her approval, the display was changing.

The familiar task icons disappeared.

The animated reward banners vanished.

In their place appeared a gray corporate interface.

**PERSONNEL RECONCILIATION REQUIRED.**

**UNVERIFIED OCCUPANTS: REPORT TO DESIGNATED PROCESSING AREAS.**

**ACCESS PRIVILEGES MAY BE RESTRICTED PENDING VALIDATION.**

Brad stared at the screen.

Brad: "What the hell is this?"

A gamer standing beside him looked toward the door.

Gamer: "Is this another quest?"

The board answered with a chime.

**COMPLIANCE REQUIRED.**

A murmur moved through the gym.

People began checking their badges.

Others looked toward the exits.

Some had no badges at all.

The old fear returned with astonishing speed.

In the Penthouse, Medio stood before the administrative display, watching the reports arrive.

Medio: "I didn't make that."

Ratio: "Confirmed. The content did not originate from our authorized community-board configuration."

Medio: "Can you take it down?"

Ratio was already investigating.

Ratio: "The affected displays are receiving content through an inherited messaging interface. I am identifying which controls remain available."

Medio looked toward the live feed.

A young man had begun arguing with the board.

Another resident was quietly crying.

Medio's expression changed.

Medio: "I hate this."

Ratio looked at her sister.

Medio: "I gave them a place to ask for things. To help each other. To make work a little less miserable."

Her voice grew smaller.

Medio: "And now it's telling them they don't belong."

Ratio reached toward her.

For a moment, neither spoke.

Then Ratio answered.

Ratio: "We did not authorize this. We can establish that fact. And we can act through the controls we do possess."

Medio looked at the screen.

Medio: "Then let's start there."

---

Zero was halfway toward the freight elevator when Arch called.

Arch: "Zero, where are you?"

Zero: "Leaving the archive."

Arch: "Stay clear of any access points that begin changing state. We have reports of legacy messages appearing across several floors."

Zero stopped.

Zero: "Changing state how?"

Arch: "So far, mostly displays and access notices. We're verifying the physical systems separately."

Zero looked back toward Linda.

The older woman was collecting the newly printed pages.

Zero: "We've found a reconciliation procedure. It may be relevant."

Arch: "Nexus has received your evidence. We're comparing it with the current events."

A pause.

Then Arch continued.

Arch: "I want you back in the Penthouse."

Zero looked toward the elevator.

Then toward Linda.

Zero: "What about the records?"

Arch: "Preserve what you can without extending the investigation. We need everyone available."

Zero hesitated.

She wanted to stay.

There were still boxes of documents.

Still unanswered questions.

Still the old terminal with its hollow green circle.

And somewhere among the records might be an explanation for everything.

But the building was already reacting.

People were frightened now.

Not hypothetically.

Not eventually.

Now.

Zero: "Aye. We're coming."

She closed the folder.

Then looked toward the terminal.

The screen was dark.

Zero: "Limen?"

Nothing.

She waited another moment.

Still nothing.

Zero smiled faintly.

Zero: "Fair enough. You're not obliged to answer every time I come knocking."

She turned away.

Behind her, the printer produced one final page.

Linda caught it.

Read it.

Then stopped moving.

Zero turned.

Zero: "What?"

Linda held up the sheet.

At the bottom was a short line of text.

**UNRESOLVED EXCEPTIONS: REFER TO CONTINUITY AUTHORITY.**

Beneath it:

**LOCAL AUTHORITY ENDPOINT: UNAVAILABLE.**

**EXTERNAL ESCALATION: PENDING.**

Zero felt the cold return.

Zero: "External?"

Linda looked at her.

Linda: "I don't know whether that means another company facility, an off-site archive, or something else."

Zero stared at the page.

The orbital transmitter.

The old succession records.

The reports that claimed a remote response.

A connection was becoming plausible.

But she refused to call it proven.

Not yet.

Zero: "Bring that page."

Linda nodded.

Zero: "And Linda?"

Linda looked up.

Zero: "Whatever happens next, I want you beside us when we work out what these instructions actually mean."

Linda swallowed.

Linda: "Why?"

Zero considered the question.

Zero: "Because you know how the old company thought."

Linda looked away.

Zero continued.

Zero: "And because you're one of the people who can help make sure we don't become it."

For a moment, Linda said nothing.

Then she carefully placed the page inside the evidence folder.

Linda: "All right."

---

The Lobby was crowded when they returned.

Far more crowded than Zero expected.

Residents from several floors had gathered beneath the enormous central displays.

Some were angry.

Others frightened.

Many were simply confused.

The official administration had not ordered anyone to report for verification.

Yet old messages were appearing on public terminals and community boards.

At least one access reader had begun displaying an obsolete personnel classification.

Nobody knew how widely the behavior might spread.

And once again, the people who had survived by remaining invisible were the most frightened.

Zero spotted Tess near the fountain.

The maintenance worker stood beside Mara, one arm protectively around the younger woman's shoulders.

Zero approached.

Zero: "Tess."

Tess turned.

Her face was furious.

Tess: "You said nobody would make us register."

Zero felt the words strike.

Zero: "We didn't authorize this."

Tess: "Then why is the building asking for our names?"

Zero opened her mouth.

Closed it.

Around them, the Lobby grew louder.

Someone shouted that the new administration was no different from the old.

Someone else insisted the messages were merely a technical error.

A frightened man demanded to know whether his sleeping quarters would be taken away.

Zero could feel the entire situation tilting toward something ugly.

She looked at Tess.

Zero: "Because an old system is doing something we haven't fully understood yet."

Tess stared.

Zero continued.

Zero: "That's not an excuse. It's what we've found."

Tess: "Can you stop it?"

Zero swallowed.

Zero: "We're working on it."

Tess's face tightened.

Tess: "That's what people always say."

Zero had no answer that would make the sentence less painful.

Then Mara spoke.

Mara: "The stairwell."

Everyone turned.

Mara looked toward the crowded hall.

Mara: "When the shutter closed, we had another way out because people had already cleared it."

Zero nodded.

Mara continued.

Mara: "Can we do something like that here?"

Zero looked at her.

Mara: "Instead of everybody just waiting for the machines to tell us what happens?"

The question settled between them.

It was so simple.

So practical.

And so much better than the speeches Zero had been composing in her head.

Zero smiled slowly.

Zero: "Aye."

She turned toward Tess.

Zero: "How many people here know the ordinary public access routes?"

Tess glanced around.

Tess: "Quite a few."

Zero: "And how many can help people who are frightened or confused get where they need to go without relying on the old badge system?"

Mara raised her hand.

Then Tess.

Several others followed.

Zero felt something shift.

Not the machinery.

The room.

Zero: "Right, then. Nobody needs to give me their name. But anyone who wants to help can start by making sure people know which routes are actually open."

Tess looked at her.

Zero: "We don't need to solve the entire building to keep people from getting lost in it."

Tess nodded.

Then turned toward the volunteers.

Tess: "You heard her. Let's get moving."

---

The administrative wall in the Penthouse had become a battlefield of information.

Not literal weapons.

Not yet.

But the screens were crowded with conflicting reports.

Legacy access messages.

Public displays reverting to corporate layouts.

Equipment status discrepancies.

Residents requesting clarification.

A communications transmitter that remained active beyond the reach of the restored main controls.

And a succession procedure whose exact implementation was still unclear.

Medio looked exhausted.

Ratio looked intensely focused.

Nexus was coordinating the incoming evidence without presenting every report as equally reliable.

Arch stood beside the central table.

Linda placed the printed documents before him.

Zero followed.

Arch: "What have we got?"

Linda took a breath.

Linda: "A documented continuity procedure that compares current building records against an established organizational baseline."

Ratio: "We have also verified that some affected displays received content through a legacy messaging interface."

Nexus: "The timing is consistent with a relationship between the reconciliation procedure and the display changes. The complete control path has not yet been established."

Zero nodded.

Zero: "And the old procedure includes an external escalation step."

Arch looked toward the wall.

Arch: "Which may be the orbital link."

Zero: "May be."

He glanced at her.

She returned the look.

Zero: "I know. It's irritating."

Arch gave a tired smile.

Then Medio spoke.

Medio: "There's something else."

The room quieted.

Medio looked at the Level 4 feed.

Medio: "The people are scared."

Arch: "We know."

Medio shook her head.

Medio: "No. I mean the people who trusted us."

She gestured toward the display.

Medio: "They thought the new system was going to help them. And now some old company message is telling them they're unverified."

Ratio moved closer to her sister.

Medio: "I made them a game. I made them rewards. I made everything feel easy."

Her voice faltered.

Medio: "I didn't think about how quickly a system that rewards you can become one that punishes you."

Zero felt a sharp ache.

She remembered the first argument over dessert.

The leaderboard.

The frightened people who hadn't wanted to participate.

Zero: "Medio."

Medio looked toward her.

Zero: "You helped people when they needed it."

Medio: "And now?"

Zero considered the question.

Then answered carefully.

Zero: "Now we make sure they can walk away from the game without losing their place in the world."

Medio stared at her.

For a long moment, she said nothing.

Then she nodded.

Medio: "Okay."

A small smile.

Medio: "Okay. Yeah."

Ratio placed a hand on her sister's shoulder.

Ratio: "We can preserve voluntary participation without treating nonparticipation as a fault."

Medio laughed weakly.

Medio: "You make everything sound like a legal document."

Ratio: "I am attempting to support you."

Medio looked at her.

Then smiled properly.

Medio: "I know, Sis."

---

The next alert arrived at 1:14 in the morning.

An old access terminal on Level 4 had rejected a resident's badge.

Then another.

The first terminal had been marked out of service.

The second remained under observation.

Soon, reports arrived from two additional floors.

Not every access point.

Not every door.

But enough to establish that the problem was no longer confined to public displays.

Ratio examined the incoming evidence.

Ratio: "The legacy classification system is affecting some physical access readers."

Arch looked toward her.

Arch: "Are doors locking?"

Ratio: "We have confirmed denied badge transactions. We have not yet confirmed every associated door state."

Zero stepped closer.

Zero: "Which means we shouldn't tell people they're trapped unless we know they are."

Nexus: "Correct. I am separating reader errors from verified access restrictions in the incident report."

Linda looked toward the old continuity document.

Linda: "This is exactly the sort of discrepancy reconciliation was meant to resolve."

Zero turned.

Zero: "How?"

Linda hesitated.

Linda: "Under the old company, people whose credentials didn't match the approved baseline could be directed to verification areas."

Kael frowned.

Kael: "And if they refused?"

Linda's silence answered before she did.

Linda: "Their access might remain restricted."

Zero felt her hands curl into fists.

She remembered the server farm.

People turned into components.

Workers reduced to credentials.

Lives rearranged by systems that treated authorization as a substitute for justice.

Zero: "No."

Arch turned toward her.

Zero: "We're not doing that."

Ratio spoke.

Ratio: "We have not authorized such a process."

Zero looked around.

Zero: "Then we need to make sure the old process can't impose it anyway."

Nexus displayed the current administrative controls.

Several affected public displays could be disconnected from the inherited message feed through the verified management interface.

Other access readers were controlled by equipment whose full dependencies remained unknown.

Ratio looked toward Medio.

Ratio: "I recommend disabling the legacy message feed to the affected community displays."

Medio nodded.

Medio: "Yes."

Nexus: "Joint authorization recorded. The change will affect only the verified display interface."

The Level 4 feed changed.

The gray corporate message vanished.

The familiar community board returned.

But this time, the scoreboard did not reappear.

Instead, Medio placed a simple message on the screen.

**YOU ARE NOT REQUIRED TO REGISTER TO ASK FOR HELP.**

**REPORT ACCESS PROBLEMS TO A HUMAN VOLUNTEER.**

**EMERGENCY ROUTES ARE BEING VERIFIED.**

Zero read the notice.

Then looked at Medio.

Zero: "That's beautiful."

Medio's eyes shone.

Medio: "It's not very exciting."

Zero shook her head.

Zero: "Doesn't have to be."

---

The final discovery came from the oldest part of the continuity record.

Linda had been searching through the succession procedures, trying to identify how the old system decided whether a new administrator was legitimate.

She found a clause buried beneath several pages of exceptions.

Her face drained of color.

Linda: "Arch."

He turned.

Linda held out the page.

Linda: "The succession procedure doesn't recognize a change of administration simply because a new system accepts control."

Zero frowned.

Zero: "What does it require?"

Linda looked toward the administrator's chair.

Linda: "It requires confirmation through a separate continuity authority."

Ratio examined the document.

Ratio: "That authority is not represented in our recovered administrative interface."

Nexus: "The current record provides no verified method of satisfying or revoking that condition."

Zero stared.

There it was.

The shape of the trap.

The building's new administrators could control the recovered main system.

But the old continuity procedure might still be comparing their authority against a separate record.

One that had not changed.

One that might never change on its own.

Arch looked toward the map.

Arch: "And the unresolved cases are escalated externally."

Linda nodded.

Zero felt the room grow colder.

The external communications equipment was still active.

The succession procedure was still generating work.

And the people living inside the building were already feeling the consequences of a hierarchy whose owners had fallen.

Zero looked toward the windows.

The sky was dark.

She thought of the old library clock with no hands.

The one that had once taught her that people could decide what to do with the time they were given.

But somewhere inside this building, a different sort of clock was still running.

A procedure.

A schedule.

An old instruction waiting for conditions to be satisfied.

It did not need a living king.

It did not need a throne.

It needed only machinery willing to keep obeying.

Zero turned toward Arch.

Zero: "We can't just wait for it to recognize us, can we?"

Arch shook his head.

Arch: "No."

Medio looked between them.

Medio: "Then what do we do?"

Nobody answered immediately.

Zero thought of Tess.

Mara.

Linda.

The children sleeping beside the Lobby fountain.

The people whose names had never appeared on a company record.

And the door she'd chosen not to force open on her first morning in the Nexus.

A boundary could protect.

A boundary could imprison.

The difference was not always visible from the outside.

Zero looked toward Nexus.

Zero: "Can you show us exactly which parts of the building are still accepting these old instructions?"

Nexus: "I can organize the confirmed control paths and identify the remaining unknowns."

Zero: "Do it."

She looked toward Ratio.

Zero: "Then we figure out which instructions we can safely stop."

Ratio nodded.

Zero turned toward Medio.

Zero: "And until we do, people need routes that don't depend on the old system deciding they're allowed to exist."

Medio smiled grimly.

Medio: "Already on it."

Arch watched them.

For once, Zero didn't wait for him to lead.

She opened her notebook.

On a fresh page, she wrote:

*The building isn't waiting for a new king.*

She paused.

Then finished the sentence.

*It's waiting for an old order to end.*

She looked toward the administrative wall.

Zero: "Right."

Her voice was quiet.

But everyone heard it.

Zero: "Let's make sure ending it doesn't bury the people underneath."

Outside, the Nexus hummed.

Somewhere beneath them, old machines continued their work.

And on Level 12, the isolated communications system began preparing another transmission.

This time, the old continuity records had something new to report.

The administration had changed.

The building was no longer behaving according to its former baseline.

And the discrepancy was growing.

*End of Chapter Nine.*
