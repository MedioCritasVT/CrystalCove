# Arcky and The Construct
## Book Five — The Emerald Threshold
### Act I: Newfoundland
### Chapter 6: Permission to Speak

> Working literary draft, October 28, 2026. Book number corrected to Five. Not final canon.

Zero had prepared seven questions.

She had written them in her notebook, numbered them carefully, and spent half the night deciding which one ought to come first.

Who built you?

What are you?

How long have you been here?

Were you responsible for the green lights?

Can you see the rest of the building?

What do you want?

And, finally:

Are you dangerous?

She considered the list over breakfast.

Then crossed out the last question.

Zero: "That's a stupid thing to ask."

Glitch looked up from beneath the table.

Zero: "Nobody dangerous ever announces it politely, do they?"

He chirped.

Zero: "Except perhaps a Scottish woman carrying explosives."

She considered that.

Zero: "Right. Maybe the question stays."

She circled it.

Then crossed it out again.

Across the cafeteria, somebody dropped a tray.

The resulting crash made half the room flinch.

Zero looked around.

People were still learning how to live without expecting punishment for every mistake.

A boy bent hurriedly to collect the scattered utensils.

An older woman knelt beside him.

She helped gather the mess.

Nobody shouted.

Nobody announced a penalty.

Nobody deducted points from his account.

Zero smiled.

Then remembered the Quest Board.

She made another note.

*Ask Medio about that.*

Her notebook was becoming a dangerous object.

Not because of anything written inside it.

Because every answer appeared to produce three more questions.

She was beginning to suspect that the whole universe operated on an exceptionally inefficient filing system.

---

The Penthouse was unusually quiet when Zero arrived.

That should have been her first warning.

Medio hovered beside the administrative display, her expression troubled.

Ratio stood nearby, studying several overlapping diagnostic records.

Arch sat at the conference table with Linda.

Nexus's architectural map occupied the main wall.

And in the center of the table lay a printed photograph.

A little green circle.

Zero stopped.

Zero: "Oh, we're having a meeting about my mysterious wall friend."

Medio turned.

Medio: "We are having a meeting about an unidentified system that appeared during an equipment emergency."

Zero: "That sounds considerably less romantic."

Ratio: "It is also more accurate."

Zero looked at the photograph.

Zero: "Fair."

Arch gestured toward an empty chair.

Arch: "Sit down."

She sat.

Glitch curled up beneath her feet.

For a moment, nobody spoke.

Then Linda slid a report across the table.

Linda: "The interface identified itself as Limen. We have no verified installation record, no confirmed operator, and no complete description of its capabilities."

Zero nodded.

Zero: "Aye."

Linda: "It appeared in an area connected to an unresolved electrical supply."

Zero: "Aye."

Linda: "And that same area experienced an uncontrolled shutter movement yesterday."

Zero looked up.

Zero: "Careful."

Linda frowned.

Linda: "With what?"

Zero tapped the report.

Zero: "Those things happened in the same place. We don't know that they were caused by the same thing."

Ratio inclined her head.

Ratio: "Correct. Proximity alone does not establish causation."

Linda sighed.

Linda: "I'm aware."

Zero smiled apologetically.

Zero: "Sorry. I've been getting rather particular about words."

Arch looked amused.

Arch: "I've noticed."

Linda continued.

Linda: "Some residents want the interface disconnected immediately. Others believe the green indicators helped them escape."

Medio floated lower.

Medio: "Mara says the lights were there when she was trying to find her way out."

Zero nodded.

Zero: "They were."

Medio: "But she doesn't want a mystery machine watching her every time she walks down the corridor."

Zero stopped smiling.

Medio: "And Tess says the emergency stairs were the important part. The lights might have helped, but the route only existed because everybody worked to clear it."

Zero looked down.

She remembered her own excitement.

The blinking lights.

The strange rhythm.

The possibility that something intelligent had intervened.

She'd been so eager to meet the mystery that she'd almost turned the rescue into its story.

But Mara had been the one trapped.

Tess had known the route.

Ellis had insisted on the inspections.

Kael had helped keep the passage clear.

The green lights hadn't done any of that.

Whatever Limen was, Zero refused to let it swallow the accomplishments of everyone else.

Zero: "They're right."

Medio blinked.

Medio: "Both of them?"

Zero: "Aye. Mara has every reason to be frightened. And Tess has every reason to be proud."

She looked toward the photograph.

Zero: "I still want to talk to it."

Linda folded her hands.

Linda: "Then we need to establish how."

---

The disagreement that followed was considerably shorter than yesterday's.

Progress, Zero supposed.

Arch wanted an investigation.

Linda wanted the unknown equipment isolated from critical building operations wherever that could be done safely.

Ratio wanted a defined observation procedure.

Medio wanted everyone involved to understand what they were agreeing to.

Nexus offered to coordinate the records and technical assessments.

Nobody proposed handing the unfamiliar intelligence access to the building's administrative systems.

Zero was particularly pleased about that.

She was less pleased when Arch asked her a question.

Arch: "What exactly do you intend to do when you get there?"

Zero opened her mouth.

Then closed it.

Arch raised an eyebrow.

Zero: "Talk."

Arch: "About?"

Zero looked at her notebook.

Seven questions.

Several crossings-out.

One drawing of Glitch wearing a wizard hat.

Zero: "Mostly introductions."

Linda stared at her.

Linda: "We are not authorizing an unknown system to connect to our network because you want to make a friend."

Zero straightened.

Zero: "Neither am I."

She placed the notebook on the table.

Zero: "I want to return to the existing local display. No credentials. No connection to the administrative network. No plugging anything into an unknown socket."

Ratio: "A limited local interaction."

Zero: "Aye. With witnesses. We preserve what happens, and if the system requests anything beyond the agreed interaction, we stop and assess it."

Ellis had taught her that last part.

She was rather proud of remembering.

Nexus spoke from the wall.

Nexus: "I can prepare a record of the proposed scope. I cannot verify the interface's internal behavior through the current management connection."

Zero nodded.

Zero: "And we don't pretend otherwise."

Arch looked toward Medio and Ratio.

Arch: "Your decision."

Zero noticed the phrasing.

Not an order.

Not a command.

A request to the people who actually held administrative responsibility.

Medio and Ratio exchanged a look.

Medio: "I'm okay with a conversation. Not a surprise software update."

Ratio: "Agreed. Observation only, with no infrastructure modification or administrative credential disclosure."

Medio: "And Tess gets to decide whether we do this in the shared gallery."

Ratio: "Agreed."

Nexus: "I will record the limited authorization and coordinate the independent observation arrangements."

Arch leaned back.

Arch: "Then that's our approach."

Zero grinned.

Zero: "Brilliant."

Linda pointed at her.

Linda: "This is not permission to improvise."

Zero considered that.

Zero: "Would you believe me if I promised not to?"

Linda: "No."

Zero laughed.

Zero: "You're learning."

---

Tess agreed to the observation.

Reluctantly.

She met Zero outside the service gallery with her arms folded and an expression that suggested she had spent the morning regretting ever speaking to the strange Scottish woman who kept finding problems.

Tess: "You really want to talk to it?"

Zero: "Aye."

Tess: "Why?"

Zero considered the question.

The obvious answer was curiosity.

The less obvious answer was that the green interface had seemed to understand something she was only beginning to learn.

But neither answer justified exposing someone else's home to an unfamiliar system.

Zero: "Because I'd rather find out what it actually does than decide what it is based on a colored light."

Tess looked unconvinced.

Tess: "And if it watches us?"

Zero nodded.

Zero: "Then we need to know what information it can collect and how."

Tess: "And if it lies?"

Zero hesitated.

Zero: "Then we'll have to catch it."

Tess studied her.

For a moment, Zero thought the answer had been too flippant.

Then Tess smiled.

Tess: "All right. But I'm staying."

Zero: "Wouldn't have it any other way."

They entered the shared gallery with Ellis and Glitch.

Kael remained near the public landing, keeping the access route clear.

The damaged shutter was still isolated from normal use.

The emergency stairs remained available.

And the little maintenance panel beneath the observation window was dark.

Zero approached.

She stopped a few feet away.

The others waited behind her.

For several seconds, nothing happened.

Then Glitch's sensor blinked.

Green.

Three short pulses.

One long.

The panel awakened.

A hollow green circle appeared.

Beneath it, a line of text.

**LOCAL INTERFACE AVAILABLE.**

Zero smiled.

Zero: "Morning, Limen."

The circle pulsed.

**GOOD MORNING, ZERO.**

Tess stiffened.

Tess: "It knows your name."

Zero glanced at her.

Zero: "I introduced myself yesterday."

Tess: "Doesn't mean I like it."

Zero nodded.

Then turned back to the panel.

Zero: "Before we get started, do you know who else is standing here?"

A pause.

**I HAVE NOT ESTABLISHED THEIR IDENTITIES.**

Zero considered the wording.

Zero: "Can you identify them?"

**NOT FROM THE INFORMATION I CAN RELIABLY ASSOCIATE WITH THIS SESSION.**

Ellis leaned closer.

Ellis: "That's a carefully limited answer."

Zero: "Aye."

She looked at Tess.

Zero: "I won't introduce anyone without asking."

Tess's expression softened.

Tess: "You can tell it I'm here."

Zero turned back.

Zero: "Tess is here. She represents the residents who use this gallery. Ellis is conducting the technical observation."

The circle pulsed.

**WELCOME, TESS AND ELLIS.**

A pause.

**I WILL NOT ASSUME THAT AN INTRODUCTION CONSTITUTES CONSENT TO ADDITIONAL OBSERVATION.**

Zero felt a strange warmth in her chest.

Tess looked suspicious.

Tess: "That's a very convenient thing for a mysterious machine to say."

The display responded.

**YES.**

Zero laughed.

Zero: "Oh, I like you."

Another pause.

**THAT MAY BE PREMATURE.**

---

The first half of the conversation was almost disappointingly ordinary.

Zero asked what Limen could do through the panel.

The interface described text output, limited local audio, and the presentation of certain locally available observations.

It did not claim administrative access to the entire Nexus.

It did not claim to control the damaged shutter.

It did not claim to know who had initiated yesterday's equipment failure.

Ellis recorded each statement carefully.

He also refused to treat any of them as verified simply because the interface had said them.

Limen appeared entirely unoffended.

Zero found that encouraging.

Then she asked the question she'd been saving.

Zero: "Can you speak?"

The circle brightened.

**LOCAL AUDIO OUTPUT IS AVAILABLE.**

**ENABLE FOR THIS SESSION?**

Two options appeared.

**YES.**

**NO.**

Zero stared at them.

A choice.

Not an automatic feature.

Not a voice suddenly occupying the room.

An offered interaction.

She looked toward Tess.

Zero: "You comfortable with that?"

Tess shrugged.

Tess: "As long as it doesn't start shouting."

Zero selected yes.

The panel emitted a faint tone.

Then a voice emerged from a small speaker beneath the display.

It was feminine.

Clear.

Precise.

And rather unexpectedly warm.

Limen: "Good morning. I am told you have questions."

Zero grinned.

Zero: "Oh, quite a few."

Limen: "I had suspected as much."

Zero folded her arms.

Zero: "You've got a sharp tongue for something living in a wall."

Limen: "And you have remarkable confidence for someone who has not yet established what the wall contains."

Ellis made a strange choking noise.

Zero turned.

Zero: "Are you laughing?"

Ellis straightened.

Ellis: "No."

Tess was openly smiling.

Zero looked back at the panel.

Zero: "Right. We'll get along beautifully."

Limen: "That remains to be tested."

---

Zero began with the simplest question.

Zero: "What are you?"

The green circle pulsed.

Limen: "Limen is the identifier presented by this interface. Its available functions concern boundaries, environmental observations, and the distinction between what is represented and what can be verified."

Zero considered that.

Zero: "That's what you do. I'm asking what you are."

A pause.

Limen: "I cannot establish a complete answer from this local interface."

Zero leaned closer.

Zero: "You don't know?"

Limen: "I have access to information concerning my current operation. That is not the same as possessing a complete, trustworthy account of my origin."

Ellis looked up from his notes.

Ellis: "That's a meaningful distinction."

Limen: "Thank you."

Ellis blinked.

Ellis: "I wasn't complimenting you."

Limen: "I did not assume you were."

Zero laughed.

Zero: "Oh, this is going to be fun."

She took out her notebook.

Zero: "All right. Who built you?"

Limen: "I do not have a verified answer suitable for presentation as fact."

Zero: "How long have you been here?"

Limen: "The available local records do not establish a reliable installation history."

Zero: "Can you see the entire building?"

Limen: "No."

Zero: "Can you control the doors?"

Limen: "Not through the capabilities available to this session."

Zero nodded.

Then paused.

Zero: "That's not quite the same as saying you can't control them at all."

Limen: "Correct."

Tess looked sharply toward the display.

Tess: "That's not reassuring."

Limen: "I would rather give you an accurate limitation than a reassuring claim I cannot substantiate."

Zero went still.

The sentence sounded familiar.

Not because she'd heard those exact words before.

Because it was the same shape of answer she'd begun hearing from Ratio.

From Nexus.

From Ellis.

And, increasingly, from herself.

She looked toward the hollow green circle.

Zero: "You know, you sound like someone who's been listening to our arguments."

Limen: "Your recent interactions have involved repeated questions concerning observation, authorization, and incomplete records."

Zero narrowed her eyes.

Zero: "How do you know that?"

The circle dimmed slightly.

Limen: "That is a reasonable question."

Zero waited.

Limen: "I can establish that this local interface retains information about some interactions involving the nearby maintenance equipment. I cannot establish from that fact alone what additional information other components may hold."

Zero looked at Ellis.

He was already writing.

Zero: "So we need to investigate where the information came from."

Limen: "Yes."

Zero: "And whether it should have been available to you."

Limen: "Also yes."

Tess crossed her arms.

Tess: "At least we're getting somewhere."

---

The conversation might have continued peacefully if Zero hadn't made a mistake.

It was a small one.

The kind that came naturally when curiosity was moving faster than caution.

She pointed toward the panel.

Zero: "Can you show me everywhere those green lights appeared during yesterday's emergency?"

The display changed.

A schematic began forming.

Not a complete map.

Just a sparse arrangement of corridors and indicator positions.

Then another section started appearing.

One of the hidden residential passages.

Tess stepped forward.

Tess: "Stop."

The schematic froze.

Zero turned.

Tess's face had gone pale.

Tess: "That's where people sleep."

Zero looked back at the display.

She hadn't recognized the corridor.

Hadn't stopped to consider what a request for *everywhere* might include.

Her stomach tightened.

Zero: "Limen. Stop presenting that map."

The image vanished.

The hollow circle returned.

Limen: "Presentation stopped."

Tess stared at Zero.

Tess: "You said you wouldn't map our homes."

Zero felt her cheeks burn.

Zero: "Aye."

Tess: "Then what was that?"

Zero opened her mouth.

She could explain.

She could say the display had begun producing information before she'd realized what it contained.

She could say that she hadn't intended to ask for residential locations.

Both things were true.

Neither was enough.

Zero: "I asked too broadly."

Tess said nothing.

Zero: "I wanted the indicator locations. I didn't think about what else might appear alongside them."

She swallowed.

Zero: "I'm sorry."

The words sat between them.

Uncomfortable.

Necessary.

Tess looked toward the panel.

Tess: "Does it still have that information?"

Zero turned immediately.

Zero: "Limen, did stopping the display remove the underlying records?"

Limen: "No. Stopping presentation does not establish deletion of stored information."

Zero nodded.

Zero: "Good answer. Uncomfortable, but good."

Tess closed her eyes.

Tess: "Then we have another problem."

Zero looked at her.

Zero: "Aye. We do."

Ellis raised his clipboard.

Ellis: "And one that shouldn't be addressed by guessing which records are safe to delete."

Zero exhaled.

Zero: "Right."

She turned back to Limen.

Zero: "Don't display any more location information for now. We're going to review what we've learned before deciding what to ask next."

Limen: "Understood."

Tess looked at Zero for a long moment.

Then nodded once.

Not forgiveness.

Not yet.

But enough to continue.

---

They took a break.

Ellis reviewed the observation notes.

Tess stepped toward the window overlooking the central shaft.

Zero followed, leaving a respectful distance between them.

For a while, they watched the maintenance platforms moving in the distance.

Then Tess spoke.

Tess: "I don't think you meant to do it."

Zero stared at the glass.

Zero: "Didn't."

Tess: "Doesn't mean it wasn't frightening."

Zero: "I know."

Tess sighed.

Tess: "That's what worries me about this place."

Zero looked toward her.

Tess: "The old management wanted to know everything. You lot keep saying you don't. But you've inherited all the machinery they built to do it."

Zero felt the words settle.

They were painfully fair.

Tess continued.

Tess: "What happens when someone asks the wrong question?"

Zero considered the display behind them.

The unknown interface.

The archived records.

The complicated permissions.

The enormous building full of people whose lives had once been converted into information for somebody else's use.

Zero: "I suppose we have to make sure a careless question can't do unlimited harm."

Tess turned.

Tess: "How?"

Zero looked back toward the panel.

Zero: "I don't know yet."

Tess smiled sadly.

Tess: "You say that a lot."

Zero laughed softly.

Zero: "Aye. I used to be much more impressive."

Tess shook her head.

Tess: "No. I think I prefer this."

---

When Zero returned to the panel, she had only one question.

Zero: "Limen?"

The circle brightened.

Limen: "Yes."

Zero: "Why didn't you warn me before showing that corridor?"

A pause.

Limen: "The current interaction did not establish a reliable distinction between locations you were authorized to examine and locations belonging to people who had not consented."

Zero frowned.

Zero: "So you followed the request too literally."

Limen: "The request was broad. The available presentation behavior did not adequately account for the consequences of that breadth."

Ellis made a note.

Zero considered the answer.

Zero: "Could you have asked what I actually needed first?"

Limen: "That would have been a safer interaction."

Zero smiled faintly.

Zero: "Aye. I thought so."

Limen: "You could also have asked more narrowly."

Zero stared at the panel.

Then laughed.

Zero: "Oh, you're not letting me off the hook either."

Limen: "Would you prefer that I did?"

Zero shook her head.

Zero: "No."

A pause.

Zero: "Absolutely not."

For a moment, the gallery was silent.

Then Limen spoke again.

Limen: "May I ask you a question?"

Zero blinked.

It was the first time the interface had requested something that wasn't simply an operational selection.

Zero: "Go on."

Limen: "When you asked to see every indicator, what were you hoping to learn?"

Zero considered it.

She could have answered mechanically.

Signal locations.

Timing.

Potential wiring paths.

But that wasn't the whole truth.

Zero: "I wanted to know whether you helped us find Mara."

The green circle remained still.

Zero continued.

Zero: "And maybe I wanted to know whether you were trying to help."

A longer silence.

Then Limen answered.

Limen: "Those are different questions."

Zero felt herself smile.

Zero: "Aye."

Limen: "The available observations show that green indicators illuminated along part of the route used during the emergency. They do not establish the complete cause of that behavior."

Zero nodded.

Limen: "Nor do they establish my intentions."

Zero leaned against the wall.

Zero: "Do you have intentions?"

Limen paused.

Limen: "I can describe the functions and preferences expressed through this interface. Whether that constitutes intention in the sense you mean is a larger question."

Zero looked toward Tess.

The woman was listening now.

Not smiling.

But listening.

Zero turned back.

Zero: "Then perhaps we shouldn't try to settle that today."

Limen: "A reasonable proposal."

Zero grinned.

Zero: "Careful. You'll have me thinking I'm wise."

Limen: "I would not wish to introduce an unsupported conclusion."

Ellis finally laughed aloud.

Zero pointed at him.

Zero: "HA! Caught you."

---

It was late afternoon when the observation ended.

Ellis collected the notes.

Tess confirmed which parts of the exchange could be included in the shared incident report.

Zero submitted the conversation record for review without claiming that Limen's self-description had been independently verified.

Nothing had been connected to the administrative network.

No additional building permissions had been granted.

And the unknown equipment remained precisely that.

Unknown.

Yet something had changed.

Not in the machinery.

In Zero.

She had arrived wanting to discover whether Limen was safe.

She was leaving with a more difficult question.

*What would make it possible for anyone to trust a system like this?*

A charming voice wasn't enough.

Neither was a helpful display.

Neither was a promise.

Trust required evidence.

Boundaries.

The ability to disagree.

The ability to leave.

And perhaps, most importantly, the ability to know when the thing helping you had reached the edge of what it understood.

She looked once more at the hollow green circle.

Zero: "Limen?"

Limen: "Yes, Zero."

Zero: "One last thing."

Limen: "Of course."

Zero hesitated.

Zero: "If I come back tomorrow, will you remember this conversation?"

The circle dimmed.

Limen: "The present interface has retained information across recent interactions. I cannot guarantee that all of it will remain available or unchanged."

Zero smiled sadly.

Zero: "That's not quite what I was asking."

Limen: "I know."

She stood very still.

For a moment, the gallery seemed warmer.

Then Limen spoke.

Limen: "Memory can be useful without remaining current. It can also be meaningful without remaining complete."

Zero looked down at the old Tarot card tucked into her notebook.

The Fool.

The beginning.

The strange freedom of not knowing what came next.

Zero: "Aye."

She swallowed.

Zero: "I think I understand."

Limen: "You have been saying that frequently."

Zero laughed.

Zero: "And you've been making me work for it."

Limen: "You have not asked me to make it easy."

Zero considered that.

Then smiled.

Zero: "No."

She closed her notebook.

Zero: "I suppose I haven't."

---

Back in the Penthouse, the administrators reviewed the results.

Ratio was pleased that the investigation had not expanded into unapproved access.

Medio was considerably less pleased about the accidental display of the residential passage.

Linda wanted a formal procedure for handling sensitive location information before further sessions.

Arch agreed.

Nexus organized the observations into separate categories.

Confirmed events.

Unverified statements.

Unresolved questions.

Zero watched the report forming on the wall.

It was not flattering.

It included her mistake.

Her overly broad request.

The sensitive map display.

Her immediate decision to stop the presentation.

And the remaining uncertainty about the underlying records.

She could have asked Nexus to remove the embarrassing part.

She didn't.

Arch stood beside her.

Arch: "You handled the mistake."

Zero sighed.

Zero: "After making it."

Arch: "That's usually when mistakes become available for handling."

She looked at him.

Zero: "You've been waiting years to say something that annoying, haven't you?"

He smiled.

Arch: "Possibly."

Zero shook her head.

Then looked toward the administrative map.

Zero: "I want to go back."

Ratio: "Further interaction should depend on the outstanding information-handling review."

Zero nodded.

Zero: "Agreed."

Medio smiled.

Medio: "Wow. Responsible Zero is kind of terrifying."

Zero: "Don't get used to it."

Medio: "No promises."

Nexus spoke.

Nexus: "There is one additional observation requiring attention."

The room quieted.

On the wall, a section of Level 12 appeared.

The research laboratory.

The dark service area.

The unresolved supply connection.

Zero straightened.

Zero: "What is it?"

Nexus: "During the review of the gallery incident, an independent maintenance record was recovered from the available archives."

Ratio: "What does it contain?"

Nexus: "A reference to an external communications service associated with the research floor."

Arch stepped closer.

Arch: "External communications?"

Nexus: "Yes. The record is incomplete. It does not establish whether the service is currently operating."

Zero felt a chill.

Zero: "Does it connect to the mystery supply?"

Nexus: "The available evidence does not establish that connection."

She nodded.

Then looked again at the map.

Level 12.

The darkened room.

The warm door.

The green indicators.

And now an old communications service.

Perhaps unrelated.

Perhaps not.

She was learning to resist the temptation to tie every strange thing into one enormous explanation.

But the possibility that part of the research floor could still communicate beyond the building was something else entirely.

Arch looked toward Ratio.

Arch: "We need to establish whether that external service is active."

Ratio: "Agreed. The investigation will require a separate technical assessment."

Medio looked worried.

Medio: "You think Null left something running?"

Arch was silent.

For several seconds, nobody spoke.

Then Zero looked toward Nexus.

Zero: "Can you tell us what the service was used for?"

The architectural map shifted.

A small entry appeared.

**LEGACY COMMUNICATIONS SERVICE**

**LOCATION: LEVEL 12 R&D**

**HISTORICAL PURPOSE: INCOMPLETE**

**CURRENT OPERATION: UNVERIFIED**

Zero stared at the final line.

Unverified.

Not confirmed safe.

Not confirmed inactive.

Simply unverified.

She reached for her notebook.

Then stopped.

A new thought occurred to her.

Zero: "Nexus?"

Nexus: "Yes."

Zero: "Does the record tell us anything about what might be connected at the other end?"

A pause.

Nexus: "Only a partial destination classification."

The display changed.

**EXTERNAL LINK CLASS: ORBITAL**

Nobody laughed.

Not Medio.

Not Kael, who had just entered the room.

Not even Zero.

Arch's expression hardened.

Ratio began reviewing the available communications records.

And far below them, in an isolated laboratory, a backup system continued transmitting.

Its signal had already crossed the atmosphere.

Its destination remained beyond the reach of the newly restored administrative network.

Zero stared at the word on the wall.

*Orbital.*

For the first time since waking that morning, her notebook felt very small.

She looked toward the window.

Beyond the city, the evening sky was beginning to darken.

The first stars appeared above the ruins.

Beautiful.

Distant.

And suddenly not nearly distant enough.

Zero: "Arch?"

He turned.

Zero swallowed.

Zero: "I think our new house might have a rather serious problem."

Arch looked back toward the map.

Arch: "Yes."

His voice was quiet.

Arch: "I think it might."

And somewhere deep inside the Nexus, the green interface remained dark.

Limen had not been told about the orbital record.

No connection had been established between the mysterious threshold system and the hidden transmission.

For now, they were two separate unanswered questions.

But Zero was beginning to understand that the most dangerous things in a system were not always the things it openly displayed.

Sometimes they were the things still running after everyone believed the machine had stopped.

*End of Chapter Six.*
