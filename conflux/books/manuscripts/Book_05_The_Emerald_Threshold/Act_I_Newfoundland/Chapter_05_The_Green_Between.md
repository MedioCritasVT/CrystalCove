# Arcky and The Construct
## Book Five — The Emerald Threshold
### Act I: Newfoundland
### Chapter 5: The Green Between

> Working literary draft. Source: edited Chapter 5 writing block (October 28, 2026); book number corrected to Five. Not approved canon.

The trouble with mysteries, Zero had decided, was that they became considerably less charming when they started keeping appointments.

At precisely 8:17 in the morning, Glitch began barking at a wall.

Not an interesting wall.

Not a suspicious wall.

A perfectly ordinary stretch of concrete between the kitchen and a maintenance closet.

Zero stood in the corridor holding a piece of toast.

Zero: "Lad, if there's another murderous appliance behind there, I'm going back to bed."

Glitch barked again.

A little green light flashed across his sensor cluster.

Three short pulses.

One long.

Then darkness.

Zero stopped chewing.

Zero: "Oh, you sneaky wee bastard."

She set the toast on a nearby crate and took out her scanner.

There was nothing particularly unusual about the electrical readings.

A low, ordinary background hum.

Some residual interference from the kitchen equipment.

And beneath it, a faint repeating signal.

She waited.

Three short pulses.

One long.

Then nothing.

Zero checked the time.

8:17.

She opened yesterday's inspection report.

The green signal Glitch had detected near the service shutter had occurred at almost the same interval.

She compared the recordings.

Same pattern.

Not identical strength.

Not necessarily the same source.

But close enough to make her stomach flutter.

Zero: "Well, hello again."

Glitch tilted his head.

Zero: "Don't look so pleased with yourself. You're the one who's been keeping secrets."

He barked.

Zero: "Aye, fair. You're a dog. That's practically your profession."

She tapped her communicator.

Zero: "Ratio? Darling? I've got a question."

Ratio: "Good morning, Zero."

Zero: "Morning. Does the kitchen maintenance corridor have any connection to Level Twelve?"

Ratio: "The documented building topology does not show a dedicated connection between those locations."

Zero sighed.

Zero: "I knew you were going to say that."

Ratio: "Would you like me to examine the available equipment records?"

Zero: "Please."

A brief pause.

Ratio: "There are several shared service pathways. Their complete physical routes are not verified."

Zero grinned.

Zero: "Now we're talking."

Medio appeared on the communicator, wearing a tiny holographic detective hat.

Medio: "DID SOMEBODY SAY MYSTERY?"

Zero closed her eyes.

Zero: "Where did you get that hat?"

Medio: "I made it."

Zero: "Of course you did."

Medio: "I also made one for Ratio."

Ratio: "I declined to wear it."

Medio: "She looks adorable in it."

Ratio: "That statement is unsupported."

Zero laughed.

Then the green light flashed again.

Glitch turned sharply toward the wall.

And somewhere behind the concrete, something clicked.

The sound was small.

Mechanical.

Almost insignificant.

But Zero heard it.

Zero: "Girls?"

Medio's expression changed.

Medio: "Yeah?"

Zero: "I think something just answered."

---

By 9:03, Zero had gathered what she insisted was an expedition.

Ellis called it an inspection.

Tess called it an inconvenience.

Kael called it an excuse to avoid carrying furniture.

Glitch appeared delighted by the entire arrangement.

They assembled in the service gallery with a portable inspection light, printed equipment diagrams, and authorization for a nonintrusive survey of the accessible service passages.

Nothing was to be opened without additional approval.

Nothing was to be energized.

Nothing was to be disconnected.

Ellis had insisted on all three conditions.

Zero had agreed.

Mostly because she was beginning to suspect that Ellis was the sort of person who would physically carry her out of a room if she became too enthusiastic.

Ellis: "I want to be absolutely clear. We're identifying the route of an unknown service connection. We're not troubleshooting it live."

Zero: "Aye."

Ellis: "We're not testing whether anything responds to commands."

Zero: "Aye."

Ellis: "And we are absolutely not touching equipment simply because it glows an interesting color."

Zero looked offended.

Zero: "That happened once."

Kael: "Four times."

Zero: "Traitor."

Tess folded her arms.

Tess: "Can we go?"

They went.

The gallery felt different from yesterday.

Not because anything had changed.

Because Zero was beginning to recognize it.

The old pipes.

The painted arrows.

The handmade stars hanging from the ventilation grille.

The little forest mural with its improbable squirrel.

A place stopped being entirely unfamiliar once you knew where people had left pieces of themselves.

Glitch trotted ahead.

His sensor cluster rotated slowly as he passed beneath the exposed conduits.

Then he stopped.

Green.

Green.

Green.

A longer pulse.

Zero raised her scanner.

Zero: "There."

Ellis examined the accessible cable markings.

Ellis: "That's an old signal conduit."

Zero: "Can you identify its purpose?"

Ellis: "Not from here."

Kael leaned closer.

Kael: "Looks like an ordinary cable run."

Tess: "That's because it is an ordinary cable run."

Zero looked at her.

Zero: "You know it?"

Tess shrugged.

Tess: "It's always been there. Nobody bothered with it because it didn't connect to anything we used."

Zero considered that.

Zero: "Did it ever have lights?"

Tess frowned.

Tess: "What?"

Zero pointed upward.

At first there was nothing to see.

Then, beneath a layer of dust, a little green indicator appeared inside a recessed fixture.

Three short pulses.

One long.

Tess stepped backward.

Tess: "No."

Zero: "No, you haven't seen it?"

Tess: "No, it didn't do that."

Ellis crouched to examine the fixture without disturbing it.

Ellis: "This isn't connected to the lighting circuit we inspected."

Zero felt the familiar rush of excitement.

Zero: "Then what's powering it?"

Ellis: "We don't know."

Zero smiled.

Zero: "I am beginning to adore those three words."

A soft voice came from her communicator.

Nexus: "I have identified a discrepancy."

Zero straightened.

Zero: "Go on."

Nexus: "The location of the observed indicator does not correspond to a documented fixture in the available architectural records."

Kael sighed.

Kael: "Of course it doesn't."

Nexus: "However, the current observation can be recorded without assigning the fixture an unverified function."

Zero looked at the little green light.

Zero: "Then do that."

Nexus: "The observation can be submitted to the administrators for inclusion in the inspection record."

Zero: "Please."

Medio joined the channel.

Medio: "Okay, but important question. Is it haunted?"

Zero: "Almost certainly."

Ratio: "There is no evidence of supernatural activity."

Zero: "That's exactly what a haunted building would want you to say."

Medio shrieked with laughter.

Even Tess smiled.

Then the lights went out.

---

It happened all at once.

The ordinary overhead fixtures died.

The ventilation fans stopped.

A low mechanical groan traveled through the wall.

For one terrible instant, Zero was back in the Penthouse, watching Mr. Null's world collapse around them.

Then an alarm sounded.

Not the building's familiar emergency chime.

Something older.

A harsh, repeating metallic tone that seemed to come from inside the concrete itself.

Ellis moved immediately.

Ellis: "Everyone away from the shutter!"

Zero turned.

At the far end of the gallery, the heavy steel barrier they had inspected yesterday began descending.

Slowly.

Unevenly.

Its motor strained against something unseen.

Tess went pale.

Tess: "That's never moved on its own before."

Kael: "What the hell is happening?"

Zero grabbed her communicator.

Zero: "Ratio! We've got movement on the damaged shutter! The gallery lights have failed!"

Ratio answered at once.

Ratio: "The main building system has not issued a shutter movement command."

Zero: "Well, somebody bloody has!"

Nexus: "An independent equipment state change has been reported. The initiating source is not identified."

Medio: "Zero, get everybody out!"

Zero turned toward the cleared emergency stairs.

The route they had opened yesterday.

The route that had seemed like such a small victory.

Now it was the only known usable exit from this section.

Zero: "Tess! Take Kael and Ellis toward the stairs!"

Tess: "What about you?"

Zero: "I'm right behind you!"

Ellis stopped.

Ellis: "Nobody touches the shutter controls. We don't know what is powering it!"

Zero: "Understood!"

They started moving.

Then someone screamed.

From the other side of the descending shutter.

A human voice.

Female.

Young.

Mara: "HELP! SOMEBODY!"

Tess froze.

Tess: "Mara?"

The scream came again.

Mara: "THE DOOR'S CLOSING!"

Zero's heart lurched.

The steel barrier was already more than halfway down.

Beyond it lay an adjoining maintenance passage.

A place the official maps barely acknowledged.

Tess took a step toward the shutter.

Zero caught her shoulder.

Zero: "No. We use the stairs."

Tess: "She's on the wrong side!"

Zero looked at the route.

The diagram.

The corridor.

The little empty place where the map stopped.

She forced herself to think.

The alternate stairwell connected to the public service landing.

If Mara could reach the adjoining corridor from the other side, there might be a way around.

But *might* was a dangerous word when somebody was trapped.

Zero raised the communicator.

Zero: "Nexus! I need the nearest confirmed escape route from the opposite side of this shutter!"

Nexus: "The available architectural data does not establish a complete route from that position."

Zero swallowed.

Zero: "Then what do we know?"

Nexus: "The cleared stairwell connects to the public service landing. The adjoining passage has not been surveyed."

Zero looked toward the dark.

Mara was still shouting.

The shutter struck the floor with a tremendous crash.

Silence followed.

Then three muffled blows against the steel.

Tess covered her mouth.

Tess: "She's trapped."

Zero felt the old instinct rise.

Break the door.

Force the mechanism.

Make something happen.

Anything.

Her hand moved toward her tools.

Then stopped.

She remembered Ellis.

Unknown supply.

Unverified isolation.

A machine capable of moving without the main building's command.

The last thing Mara needed was a second disaster caused by somebody trying to be heroic.

Zero lowered her hand.

Zero: "We are not opening that thing blind."

Tess stared at her.

Tess: "Then what are we doing?"

Zero looked at Glitch.

His sensor cluster was blinking green.

Three short pulses.

One long.

And above the shutter, the little recessed indicator was blinking too.

Only now, there was another light.

Further down the corridor.

Then another.

Zero turned slowly.

A sequence of tiny green indicators had awakened along the wall.

Not bright enough to illuminate the passage.

Just visible enough to follow.

They led toward the emergency stairs.

Zero's pulse quickened.

Zero: "Well, that's new."

---

The green lights did not behave like ordinary emergency lighting.

They didn't illuminate every exit.

They didn't cover the entire floor.

They appeared only along one route.

And when Zero moved toward the stairs, another illuminated ahead.

Ellis looked deeply unhappy.

Ellis: "We are not treating an unidentified indicator as proof of a safe route."

Zero: "Agreed."

Kael glanced between them.

Kael: "Then why are we following it?"

Zero: "Because the stairs are already a route we've inspected. The lights are pointing toward something we know."

She looked back at the shutter.

Zero: "And somebody has to find Mara."

Tess seized her arm.

Tess: "I'll show you the other side. There's a public corridor that might reach the maintenance landing."

Zero nodded.

Zero: "You lead. Kael, stay with Ellis and keep the passage clear."

Kael looked about to protest.

Then he saw Zero's face.

Kael: "Right."

They hurried toward the stairs.

Glitch bounded ahead, his metal feet striking sparks from the grated landing.

The green lights followed them.

Or rather, they seemed to be appearing in the direction they were already moving.

Zero wasn't prepared to claim anything more.

Not yet.

They reached the public landing.

Tess pointed toward an unmarked service door.

Tess: "Through there."

Zero examined the door.

It wasn't locked.

She opened it.

Beyond lay a narrow passage lined with pipes.

The air was warmer.

Somewhere ahead, a motor continued running.

They moved carefully.

Then Zero heard it.

A faint voice.

Mara: "Hello?"

Zero stopped.

Zero: "Mara? Can you hear me?"

Mara: "YES!"

Tess shouted.

Tess: "Mara, it's Tess! Stay where you are!"

A sob of relief echoed from the darkness.

They rounded the corner.

Mara stood beside a stack of maintenance crates, clutching a battered toolbox.

She was unharmed.

Terrified.

But unharmed.

Zero felt her knees weaken.

Zero: "Oh, thank every god you've got."

Mara stared at her.

Mara: "I couldn't get back through! Everything started closing!"

Zero approached slowly.

Zero: "You're all right. We're getting out."

Mara looked past her.

At the little green lights along the corridor.

Mara: "What are those?"

Zero followed her gaze.

One indicator blinked beside the exit.

Then another.

Three short pulses.

One long.

Zero: "I haven't the faintest idea, love."

Mara gave a shaky laugh.

Zero: "But I'm beginning to think somebody wanted us to find you."

---

They returned to the Lobby twenty minutes later.

The emergency had spread through the building faster than Zero would have liked.

Volunteers were already gathering around the service entrance.

Linda had organized a temporary safety perimeter.

Medio was trying to keep frightened residents calm without pretending that everything was fine.

Ratio was coordinating inspections of affected equipment.

And Nexus was quietly assembling the available observations into a timeline.

Zero found Arch beside the administration display.

He looked at her.

Then at Mara.

Then at Glitch.

Arch: "Everyone accounted for?"

Zero nodded.

Zero: "Everyone involved in that passage, aye. We still need to verify the other affected areas."

Arch exhaled.

Arch: "Good."

He examined the incident display.

Arch: "What triggered the shutter?"

Zero looked toward Nexus.

Nexus: "The initiating command has not been identified. The main administration issued no corresponding operation."

Arch's face darkened.

Arch: "Could it have been an automatic safety function?"

Nexus: "Possibly. The observed behavior is insufficient to establish its purpose."

Ratio appeared beside the display.

Ratio: "I have restricted further operation of the affected gallery through the controls we can verify. However, we have not established independent control over the unknown supply."

Zero folded her arms.

Zero: "Which means there's a part of this building still doing things without asking its new administrators."

Arch nodded slowly.

Arch: "Yes."

Medio looked troubled.

Medio: "I thought we'd taken control."

Ratio: "We assumed control of the systems exposed through the recovered administrative interface."

Zero looked at her.

Zero: "Not necessarily everything physically connected to the building."

Ratio: "Correct."

Silence settled over them.

For the first time since Mr. Null's defeat, the full meaning of their victory seemed to shift.

They had captured the Nexus.

But capture was not comprehension.

And administrative control was not proof that every independent machine had been brought under supervision.

Arch turned toward the map.

Arch: "We need to identify what remains outside the current system."

Zero nodded.

Zero: "Aye. But first, I want to know about those green lights."

---

The recorded footage was strange.

Not supernatural.

Not impossible.

Simply strange.

In one camera view, the ordinary lights failed.

In another, the emergency shutter began descending.

And in a third, captured by Glitch's onboard camera, a sequence of small green indicators appeared along the service corridor.

Zero watched the recording twice.

Then a third time.

Medio leaned over her shoulder.

Medio: "Okay. That is officially creepy."

Ratio: "The indicators may belong to an independent safety or guidance system."

Zero: "Then why isn't it on the map?"

Ratio: "Unknown."

Zero laughed softly.

Zero: "I walked into that one."

Nexus spoke.

Nexus: "There is another distinction worth preserving."

Zero looked up.

Zero: "Go on."

Nexus: "The indicators appeared to correspond with the route used during the evacuation. We have not established whether they were directing the movement, responding to it, or operating according to another condition."

Zero nodded slowly.

Zero: "So we know what happened. Not why."

Nexus: "Correct."

Zero leaned back.

Then glanced toward Arch.

Zero: "If we're going to understand this place, we need to understand the systems that don't answer to us."

Arch: "Agreed."

She looked back at the recording.

Green.

Dark.

Green.

Dark.

Something about the rhythm bothered her.

Not because it seemed threatening.

Because it seemed deliberate.

She had spent years studying signals.

Bad signals.

Broken signals.

Signals disguised as other things.

A rhythm could be coincidence.

A repeated pattern could be machinery.

But sometimes a pattern meant someone had designed a way to communicate.

She wasn't ready to decide which this was.

Yet.

---

That evening, Zero returned to the service gallery.

Not alone.

Ellis accompanied her, along with Tess and a building safety volunteer.

They stayed outside the isolated shutter area, reviewing the accessible indicators and taking measurements from a safe location.

The unknown supply remained unresolved.

The emergency route remained open.

And the green lights were dark.

Zero stood beside the observation window overlooking the hidden central shaft.

The sunlight had begun fading.

Far below, maintenance platforms moved through the enormous interior.

Some carrying people.

Others carrying equipment.

A few moving without anyone aboard.

The building seemed to be settling into its evening rhythms.

Ellis checked his notes.

Ellis: "Whatever those indicators are, they aren't currently active."

Zero: "Aye."

Ellis: "And we still don't have a circuit identification."

Zero: "Aye."

Ellis: "You don't seem particularly discouraged."

Zero smiled.

Zero: "I've spent my entire life chasing things that don't make sense. This is practically a holiday."

Ellis gave her a doubtful look.

Then returned to his measurements.

Zero leaned against the window.

Glitch sat beside her.

She watched the city beyond the glass.

And remembered the High Priestess.

The veil.

The pillars.

The quiet suggestion that knowledge sometimes waited behind a boundary.

She'd always loved that card.

Not because it promised secret power.

But because it reminded her that mystery was not the same thing as emptiness.

Something could remain undiscovered without existing for the sole purpose of being discovered.

Zero looked down.

Glitch's green indicator flashed.

Once.

She crouched.

Zero: "Hello again."

The indicator flashed twice.

Zero frowned.

Zero: "That's different."

The recessed light above the window awakened.

Green.

Then another beside the service conduit.

But this time the lights didn't form a route.

They remained still.

Waiting.

Zero looked around.

Ellis was examining the opposite wall.

Tess was discussing the emergency notice with the volunteer.

Nobody else seemed to have noticed.

Zero stepped toward the nearest indicator.

A small maintenance display was mounted beneath it.

The display had been dark every time she'd passed.

Now a single line of text appeared.

**OBSERVER DETECTED.**

Zero stopped breathing.

The words changed.

**LOCAL INTERFACE AVAILABLE.**

She stared.

Then slowly reached for her communicator.

Zero: "Nexus?"

Nexus: "Yes, Zero."

Zero: "Are you displaying anything on the maintenance panel by the observation window?"

Nexus: "No. That device is not registered as an active endpoint in the currently available management interface."

Zero swallowed.

Zero: "Can you read it?"

Nexus: "Not through my present connection."

Zero looked back at the screen.

The words had disappeared.

In their place was a tiny green circle.

Hollow.

Unfilled.

A shape that reminded her of the ritual boundaries she'd drawn in chalk as a girl.

A circle could mean protection.

A circle could mean separation.

A circle could mean that something belonged within a space.

But a circle could also mean an opening.

A beginning.

Zero smiled nervously.

Zero: "Right, then."

A new line appeared.

**NO CHANGE TO ACCESS PERMISSIONS REQUESTED.**

Zero blinked.

She hadn't touched anything.

Hadn't entered a command.

Hadn't authenticated.

The display seemed to be telling her what it was *not* doing.

That was unusual.

Very unusual.

Zero: "Who are you?"

For several seconds, nothing happened.

Then the screen changed again.

**THAT IS A LARGER QUESTION THAN THIS INTERFACE CAN ANSWER.**

Zero stared.

Then, despite herself, laughed.

Zero: "Oh, you're cheeky."

A pause.

**OBSERVATION IS NOT AUTHORIZATION.**

Her smile faded.

The words were simple.

Almost clinical.

But they struck something deep inside her.

She remembered Tess's hidden home.

The broken shutter.

The building map.

The questions they'd argued over in the Penthouse.

The difference between seeing a thing and owning it.

Between recognizing a door and possessing the right to open it.

Zero looked at the green circle.

Zero: "Aye."

She swallowed.

Zero: "I've been learning that."

The display remained quiet.

Then another line appeared.

**YOU APPEAR TO HAVE NOTICED.**

She laughed again.

Softly this time.

Zero: "You're definitely cheeky."

Behind her, Ellis called out.

Ellis: "Zero? Are you talking to the wall?"

She turned.

Zero: "Possibly."

Ellis stared.

Zero: "Before you judge me, the wall started it."

He approached.

The green circle remained visible.

He stopped.

Ellis: "That's new."

Zero: "Aye."

He examined the panel without touching it.

Ellis: "We should document this."

Zero nodded.

Zero: "Absolutely."

She raised her scanner.

Then hesitated.

On the screen, beneath the circle, a final line appeared.

**LOCAL IDENTIFIER: LIMEN**

Zero read the word.

Slowly.

Limen.

A threshold.

A boundary.

A place between one state and another.

She had encountered the term before in her studies.

It was an interesting name for something that appeared wherever the building's maps stopped making sense.

Zero looked at the display.

Zero: "Limen, is it?"

The circle pulsed gently.

A soft green light washed across the window frame.

Then the panel went dark.

Glitch chirped.

Zero stood motionless.

For a long moment, she said nothing.

Then she smiled.

A dangerous little smile.

The sort Arch would have recognized instantly.

Zero: "Well."

She looked toward the darkened corridor.

Zero: "I think we've just met somebody."

---

High above, in the Penthouse, Nexus received the inspection report.

The newly documented maintenance interface had identified itself as Limen.

Its physical connection remained unverified.

Its capabilities were unknown.

Its authorization status was not established.

Nexus did not classify it as trusted.

Nor did she classify it as hostile.

She recorded the observation.

Preserved the evidence.

And submitted a request for further assessment to Medio and Ratio.

Elsewhere, the captured building continued its ordinary work.

The residents ate.

The emergency routes remained open.

The machinery hummed.

And in a dark laboratory on Level 12, an isolated system continued transmitting toward orbit.

The signal traveled outward.

Beyond the ruined city.

Beyond the atmosphere.

Toward something the new administrators had not yet seen.

The green interface's appearance had not established any connection to that threat.

But somewhere inside the Nexus, two very different mysteries were now unfolding.

One concerned a machine reaching outward.

The other concerned a boundary that had begun speaking.

And Zero, who had spent much of her life believing that every mystery was an invitation to open another door, had finally encountered something that seemed interested in whether she understood the door first.

She returned to her room that night with a new entry in her notebook.

*Limen.*

Beneath it she wrote:

*Not every threshold is a warning.*

She paused.

Then added:

*And not every invitation is permission.*

Zero leaned back.

Thought about the green circle.

And smiled.

Zero: "Well, then. Let's see what kind of conversation you are."

Outside, the Nexus kept breathing.

*End of Chapter Five.*
