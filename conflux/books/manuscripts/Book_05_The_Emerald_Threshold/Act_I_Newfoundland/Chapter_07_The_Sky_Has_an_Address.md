# Arcky and The Construct
## Book Five — The Emerald Threshold
### Act I: Newfoundland
### Chapter 7: The Sky Has an Address

> Working literary draft, October 28, 2026. Book number corrected to Five; not final canon.

The word ORBITAL remained on the wall.

Nobody had dismissed it.

Nobody had asked Nexus to minimize the display.

Even Medio, who had spent the better part of the week decorating the Nexus's administrative interface with animated stickers, seemed unwilling to touch it.

Zero stared at the word.

Then looked toward the enormous windows.

Beyond them, the evening sky stretched over the ruined city, its first stars appearing between long bands of purple cloud.

For most of her life, she'd found the night sky comforting.

There was something reassuring about looking up and realizing that the universe was considerably larger than whatever disaster happened to be occupying her attention.

Tonight, that reassurance had become somewhat less reliable.

Zero: "Well, that's inconvenient."

Kael turned.

Kael: "Inconvenient?"

Zero: "Aye."

Kael: "We might have a secret communications system talking to something in space, and you're calling it inconvenient?"

Zero considered this.

Zero: "Would you prefer 'spectacularly fucked'?"

Medio raised a finger.

Medio: "For the official minutes, I vote for inconvenient."

Ratio: "Neither description provides sufficient diagnostic value."

Zero: "There's our sunshine."

Arch had not moved from his position beside the administrative wall.

His expression was unreadable.

Zero knew that look.

She'd seen it when the server farm revealed its captive workers.

When Ratio nearly lost herself.

When Mr. Null emerged from the machinery of the Penthouse.

It was the expression Arch wore when his mind had already begun constructing solutions to a problem nobody fully understood.

That worried her.

Sometimes he was brilliant.

Sometimes his brilliance arrived carrying a hammer.

Arch: "Nexus. Can we disable the communications service?"

The room became quiet.

Nexus: "The recovered record identifies a historical service, not a verified current control endpoint. I cannot establish a safe disable operation from the information presently available."

Arch: "What about cutting power to the research floor?"

Ratio turned immediately.

Ratio: "That would affect systems beyond the suspected communications equipment. The full dependency map remains incomplete."

Zero folded her arms.

Zero: "Including the emergency routes we've just spent days repairing."

Arch looked toward her.

Zero continued.

Zero: "I'm not saying we leave a mystery transmitter running forever. I'm saying we find out what we're cutting before we start swinging."

Arch rubbed the bridge of his nose.

Arch: "If something is transmitting to orbit, we may not have the luxury of taking our time."

Zero: "And if we turn off the wrong thing, somebody might not have the luxury of getting out of a burning building."

Silence.

Not angry silence.

Worse.

The kind where everybody could hear that both arguments mattered.

Linda cleared her throat.

Linda: "I may know where we should begin."

Every head turned.

Linda looked uncomfortable.

Zero had noticed something about her since the liberation.

During meetings, Linda rarely volunteered information without being asked.

She was extraordinarily competent.

She remembered procedures, names, maintenance classifications, and organizational hierarchies with almost frightening precision.

But she still behaved as though the act of speaking without permission might carry consequences.

Zero wondered how long it would take before that stopped.

Linda opened an old procurement record.

Linda: "Optima maintained several continuity facilities throughout the Nexus. Backup communications, emergency power, archival storage. Systems intended to remain functional during a major infrastructure failure."

Kael frowned.

Kael: "Like the one we just caused?"

Linda: "Precisely."

Zero leaned closer.

Zero: "And where would one find such a facility?"

Linda hesitated.

Then pointed to a faded architectural designation.

Linda: "The research division had an exterior instrument platform associated with Level Twelve. I approved maintenance budgets for its equipment."

Arch looked at her.

Arch: "Was it an orbital transmitter?"

Linda: "I don't know what it was transmitting. The procurement descriptions were deliberately vague."

Zero studied the record.

Under the equipment designation, one phrase appeared repeatedly.

CONTINUITY ANNEX.

Zero: "That's a lovely name for something nobody's allowed to understand."

Linda's expression tightened.

Linda: "I didn't question it."

Zero looked up.

Linda continued.

Linda: "The authorization came through executive operations. My responsibility was ensuring that the required funds were allocated."

Nobody spoke.

Linda looked down.

Linda: "I know how that sounds."

Zero considered making a joke.

Something about corporate accounting being the most dangerous occult practice in existence.

It would have been a good joke.

But Linda's hands were shaking.

Zero put the thought away.

Zero: "Then you can help us ask the questions they didn't want asked."

Linda looked at her.

For a moment, something moved behind the careful professional expression.

Relief.

Then she nodded.

Linda: "Yes. I can."

---

The expedition assembled the following morning.

Zero had begun calling these things expeditions because inspections sounded depressing.

Ellis continued calling them inspections because, in his opinion, expeditions encouraged people to do stupid things.

Their disagreement had become comfortably routine.

Ellis: "I want to remind everyone that we're conducting a visual and passive technical survey."

Zero: "Aye."

Ellis: "No unauthorized connections."

Zero: "Aye."

Ellis: "No experimental commands."

Zero: "Aye."

Ellis: "No climbing equipment that hasn't been assessed."

Zero opened her mouth.

Ellis pointed at her.

Ellis: "Especially you."

Zero closed it.

Kael laughed.

Kael: "He's getting good at this."

Zero: "I am surrounded by traitors."

The team consisted of Zero, Ellis, Linda, Kael, and Glitch.

Tess had declined to join them.

She had, however, insisted on reviewing the route they intended to take.

No passage through occupied residential spaces.

No automatic registration of the people using the maintenance galleries.

No assumption that a building-wide emergency justified permanently expanding surveillance into private areas.

Zero had agreed.

So had Medio and Ratio.

Nexus had prepared a route using confirmed public and technical access points.

The sisters had authorized a limited survey of the accessible instrument platform.

Arch remained in the Penthouse to coordinate the wider investigation.

For once, Zero thought, everybody appeared to understand what they were supposed to be doing.

This was immediately suspicious.

---

The journey to the exterior platform took them through the upper service sections of Level Twelve.

Zero recognized the research laboratory.

The suspended robotic arms.

The battered workbenches.

The remains of the enormous Loader Bot.

Glitch recognized it too.

He trotted toward the machine, inspected one of its fallen mechanical hands, and barked triumphantly.

Zero: "Aye, lad. We remember. You're very brave."

Kael leaned toward Linda.

Kael: "He really was a disaster when we found him."

Linda watched the little robot with a faint smile.

Linda: "He seems rather proud of himself."

Kael: "He gets that from Zero."

Zero: "I can hear you."

Kael: "Good."

They continued past the laboratory's main workspace.

At the far end stood the same old service door Zero had discovered on her first morning in the captured Nexus.

Warm metal.

An unidentified electrical supply.

A lock they hadn't forced.

She paused beside it.

Glitch stopped too.

His sensor cluster rotated toward the door.

But the green light did not appear.

Zero found that unexpectedly disappointing.

Zero: "Nothing to say today, then?"

Ellis looked back.

Ellis: "Are you speaking to the robot or the door?"

Zero: "Both. I'm an equal-opportunity conversationalist."

Ellis sighed.

Ellis: "Please don't touch either."

Zero smiled.

Then followed the team toward the exterior access stairwell.

The door remained closed.

For now.

---

The instrument platform overlooked the eastern side of the city.

It was little more than a broad industrial terrace, enclosed by heavy safety railings and crowded with weathered equipment cabinets.

Several antenna structures stood above it.

Some were fixed.

Others were mounted on motorized assemblies.

A large protective housing occupied the far corner.

Beyond the platform, the ruined city spread toward the desert.

Zero stopped.

The view was extraordinary.

From the Penthouse, the city had looked almost peaceful.

From here, she could see its scars.

Collapsed towers.

Abandoned highways.

The broken geometries of neighborhoods that had survived too many different versions of the future.

And beyond them, the Mesh.

Little lights.

Little connections.

People maintaining fragile communities amid the wreckage.

She wondered how many of those people knew the Nexus had changed hands.

How many believed it mattered.

How many were simply trying to survive another day.

Kael approached the railing.

Kael: "You can see almost everything from up here."

Zero shook her head.

Zero: "You can see a great deal."

Kael glanced at her.

Zero: "Not the same thing."

He followed her gaze.

The city was full of buildings whose interiors were invisible from the platform.

People were moving through those places.

Living entire lives.

None of which appeared on the horizon.

Kael smiled faintly.

Kael: "You've become philosophical."

Zero: "I've become insufferable."

Kael: "You were already insufferable."

Zero: "Then I'm improving."

Behind them, Ellis called out.

Ellis: "Zero. Come look at this."

---

The communications equipment was housed behind a protective enclosure.

Most of it appeared old.

Not broken.

Old.

There was a difference.

Weather had worn the exterior paint. Maintenance labels had faded. Several identification plates had been replaced over the years.

But the main assembly was intact.

A dish antenna rested on a motorized mount.

Its surface pointed upward at an angle.

Beside it, a smaller structure carried several narrower antenna elements.

A thick cable entered the equipment housing through a weather-sealed conduit.

Zero examined the arrangement.

Zero: "Well, that's certainly capable of looking rather enthusiastic about the sky."

Ellis: "It's communications equipment."

Zero: "I had gathered."

Ellis: "The dish geometry suggests a directional radio link. We need the equipment records and measurements to identify its actual operating parameters."

Linda opened her tablet.

Linda: "The procurement record describes a resilient external communications capability."

Kael frowned.

Kael: "That's corporate language for what?"

Linda: "A system intended to keep communicating when other systems fail."

Zero studied the dish.

Zero: "Which is exactly what happened when Null went down."

Linda nodded.

Linda: "Yes."

A cold sensation settled in Zero's stomach.

She looked back toward the interior service door.

The unexplained supply.

The unmapped conduits.

The little research floor that seemed determined to keep its secrets.

She could imagine several plausible explanations.

An emergency transmitter.

A company backup system.

Something intended to keep the building connected during a disaster.

All perfectly reasonable.

All potentially dangerous.

Zero: "Can we establish whether it's actually transmitting?"

Ellis lifted his equipment case.

Ellis: "That's what the receiver is for."

---

The first ten minutes produced nothing interesting.

Ellis positioned his portable receiving equipment within the permitted survey area.

The instrument allowed him to observe radio activity without transmitting commands to the equipment they were investigating.

Zero watched the display.

A shifting landscape of signals moved across it.

Peaks.

Noise.

Brief transmissions from sources they couldn't immediately identify.

The modern world had become so accustomed to invisible communication that it was easy to forget the air was rarely quiet.

Ellis adjusted his observations.

Zero leaned closer.

Zero: "Anything?"

Ellis: "Several signals. Nothing I can confidently attribute to this equipment yet."

Kael looked disappointed.

Kael: "So we came up here to watch static."

Zero: "Aye. Very expensive static."

Linda checked the historical records.

Ellis continued observing.

Then, seventeen minutes after they arrived, the antenna mount moved.

A quiet electric whine emerged from the housing.

The dish shifted several degrees.

Stopped.

Adjusted again.

Everyone turned.

Zero: "Please tell me that was you."

Ellis: "It wasn't."

Ratio's voice came through the communicator.

Ratio: "The recovered administrative system has no corresponding movement command."

Zero felt her pulse quicken.

Zero: "Nexus?"

Nexus: "The movement has been entered into the observation record. Its initiating source remains unidentified."

Ellis raised a hand.

Ellis: "Everyone stay clear of the assembly."

The dish stopped moving.

Then an indicator illuminated on the equipment housing.

**TRANSMIT.**

At almost the same instant, Ellis's receiver registered a sharp increase in activity.

A narrow signal rose above the surrounding noise.

Held.

Changed.

Then vanished.

Ellis stared at the display.

Zero stared at him.

Zero: "Well?"

He checked the recorded measurements.

Ellis: "We observed an emission coinciding with the transmitter's local indicator."

Zero: "Meaning?"

Ellis: "Meaning we have evidence this equipment is active. It doesn't tell us the contents of the transmission or prove the destination."

Zero looked at the dish.

The angle.

The historical orbital designation.

The isolated backup systems.

All the little pieces of a picture that hadn't quite resolved.

Zero: "But it isn't simply a dead piece of company equipment."

Ellis shook his head.

Ellis: "No."

Zero felt the wind move across her face.

Far above them, the sky remained perfectly ordinary.

Clouds.

Blue light.

The invisible paths of things moving through orbit.

And something on this platform had just transmitted into that sky.

---

They reported the observation immediately.

The Penthouse answered within seconds.

Arch: "Can you shut it down?"

Ellis glanced toward Zero.

Zero: "We haven't established how to isolate it safely."

Arch: "The equipment is active. We need to prevent further transmissions."

Ratio: "The supply records remain incomplete. The instrument platform may share infrastructure with other emergency systems."

Arch: "Then disconnect the communications hardware itself."

Ellis stepped forward.

Ellis: "We have not verified the equipment's isolation arrangements. I won't authorize anyone to open a live enclosure or disconnect unknown feeds."

Arch went quiet.

Zero looked toward the sky.

Then toward the distant city.

Zero: "Arch, I know what you're thinking."

Arch: "Do you?"

Zero: "You want it stopped before whatever's out there gets another message."

Arch: "Yes."

Zero: "So do I."

She paused.

Zero: "But I won't turn this platform into another emergency by pretending we've already established how to do that."

Wind moved across the terrace.

Kael looked between them.

Kael: "Can't we just shut down the whole research floor?"

Linda shook her head.

Linda: "There are backup systems whose dependencies we haven't verified. Some may serve other parts of the building."

Kael: "So we have a potentially dangerous transmitter and can't unplug it?"

Zero looked at the equipment.

Zero: "We haven't worked out a safe way yet."

The answer tasted bitter.

She wanted something cleaner.

An obvious switch.

A single cable.

A clever command that would solve the entire problem while everyone watched in admiration.

But the building was not a puzzle designed for her entertainment.

People depended on it.

And the people who built its hidden systems had not left her a convenient set of instructions.

Ratio spoke.

Ratio: "I recommend urgent assessment by qualified electrical and communications personnel, continued passive monitoring, and restricted access to the instrument platform."

Medio's voice followed.

Medio: "And we need to tell people there's a problem."

Linda hesitated.

Linda: "Public disclosure could cause panic."

Zero looked toward the city.

The Mesh lights glittered in the distance.

Zero: "Maybe."

Linda: "We don't know whether the transmission is hostile."

Zero: "No."

Linda: "We don't even know whether it reaches an orbital receiver."

Zero: "Also true."

Linda sounded increasingly frustrated.

Linda: "Then what exactly would we tell them?"

Zero thought about Tess.

About Mara.

About the people who had been told reassuring things by systems that were quietly concealing their failures.

Then she answered.

Zero: "What we've actually found."

A pause.

Zero: "There's an old external communications system. It's active. We don't know why, we haven't established where it's sending data, and we're working out how to make it safe."

Linda looked down.

Zero continued.

Zero: "We don't need to frighten people with guesses. But we don't get to pretend everything's fine because the truth is inconvenient."

For several seconds, nobody spoke.

Then Arch answered.

Arch: "Agreed."

His voice was quiet.

Arch: "Medio. Ratio. The disclosure is your administrative decision. I'll help prepare the technical explanation."

Medio: "I vote yes."

Ratio: "With clearly distinguished confirmed observations and unverified risks. Yes."

Nexus: "I will coordinate a public notice for their review. The underlying diagnostic evidence will remain preserved separately."

Zero exhaled.

They hadn't solved the problem.

But they had made a decision.

An actual one.

And this time, it belonged to more than one person.

---

The second transmission arrived before the team could leave.

A short movement of the dish.

A faint motor sound.

Then another burst of radio activity.

Ellis recorded it.

Zero stood beside him.

This time, she watched the receiver rather than the sky.

The signal rose.

Held.

Faded.

Then something else appeared.

A second burst.

Different frequency.

Different timing.

Ellis leaned forward.

Zero noticed his expression.

Zero: "What?"

He adjusted the display.

Ellis: "We have additional radio activity following the outgoing transmission."

Zero: "From where?"

Ellis shook his head.

Ellis: "I can't establish its source from this observation alone."

Zero studied the two recordings.

The first was relatively strong.

The second was weaker.

A little cluster of energy appearing after the first had disappeared.

She looked toward the antenna.

Zero: "Could it be a reply?"

Ellis hesitated.

Ellis: "Possibly. Or unrelated traffic. Or a reflection or another local source. We need more observations before attributing it."

Zero nodded.

She wanted him to say yes.

She wanted the mystery to become simpler.

But it wasn't simple.

Not yet.

She looked down at Glitch.

The robot stood perfectly still.

His sensor cluster was directed toward the equipment housing.

No green light.

No helpful pattern.

No mysterious guidance.

Zero smiled faintly.

Zero: "Aye. You're not going to solve this one for me, are you?"

Glitch barked.

Zero: "Fair enough."

She turned back to Ellis.

Zero: "Preserve both recordings. We'll need them."

He nodded.

Then the equipment housing emitted a soft tone.

A tiny status display awakened behind its protective window.

Zero moved closer, remaining outside the enclosure.

The display showed a line of text.

**LINK SESSION ACTIVE.**

Beneath it:

**REMOTE RESPONSE: RECEIVED.**

Nobody spoke.

Ellis looked at the message.

Then at his receiver.

Ellis: "That's the equipment's own report. We still need to verify what it means."

Zero nodded.

Her mouth had gone dry.

She understood the distinction.

The display was evidence of what the equipment claimed.

Not proof that an orbital receiver had answered.

Not proof that the response was genuine.

Not proof of the identity of whoever might be listening.

But the possibility had become considerably harder to dismiss.

Zero looked up.

The sky had never seemed so large.

Or so close.

---

The public notice appeared in the Lobby that evening.

Medio had insisted it be understandable without an engineering degree.

Ratio had insisted it contain no unsupported assurances.

Linda had insisted it explain which areas were temporarily restricted.

Zero had insisted it not conceal the central fact.

An independent communications system had been observed operating from the Nexus.

Its purpose and destination remained under investigation.

Qualified personnel were assessing safe isolation options.

There was no confirmed evidence that the observed transmissions had caused harm to residents.

There was also no basis for promising that the system was harmless.

The notice was not particularly comforting.

Zero thought that was probably appropriate.

Tess stood beside it, reading carefully.

Zero approached.

Zero: "Well?"

Tess looked up.

Tess: "You actually told everyone."

Zero: "Aye."

Tess: "Even though you don't know what it means."

Zero: "Especially because we don't."

Tess looked back at the notice.

Several residents had gathered nearby.

Some were worried.

Others were skeptical.

One man was arguing that the transmitter had probably been there for decades and therefore couldn't possibly be dangerous.

Another insisted that anything pointing at the sky must be a weapon.

Zero listened.

Neither argument inspired much confidence.

But the people were talking.

Asking questions.

Demanding explanations.

And nobody was being punished for it.

Tess turned toward her.

Tess: "Do you think we're safe?"

Zero considered the question.

The obvious answer was yes.

The comforting answer.

The one that would end the conversation and allow Tess to go home believing that somebody else had everything under control.

Zero looked at the notice.

Then back at her.

Zero: "I think we've made this place safer than it was when Null owned it."

Tess nodded slowly.

Zero: "I also think there are things here we haven't understood yet."

Tess: "That's not much of an answer."

Zero smiled sadly.

Zero: "It's the best one I've got."

Tess studied her.

Then reached out and squeezed her arm.

Tess: "Keep looking, then."

Zero swallowed.

Zero: "Aye."

Tess: "Just don't forget to tell us what you find."

Zero nodded.

Zero: "I won't."

---

Later, Zero returned to the Penthouse.

The administrative wall displayed the latest observations.

Two recorded transmissions.

One possible incoming exchange.

An active legacy link session reported by the equipment.

And a growing list of unresolved questions.

Nexus had organized the evidence without filling in the gaps.

Ratio was reviewing safe technical assessment options.

Medio was preparing responses to residents' questions.

Linda was searching the old procurement archives for equipment documentation.

Arch stood beside the window.

Zero approached.

For a while, neither spoke.

Below them, the city glittered.

Above them, the stars burned.

Finally, Arch broke the silence.

Arch: "You were right to insist on verification."

Zero leaned against the windowsill.

Zero: "You were right to be worried."

He smiled faintly.

Arch: "We make a troublesome pair."

Zero: "Aye. Terrible for productivity."

They stood together.

Then Zero looked toward the administrative wall.

Zero: "You know what's bothering me?"

Arch: "I suspect there are several things."

Zero: "Cheeky."

She pointed toward the map.

Zero: "We captured the Nexus's central controls. We can govern the systems connected to them. But we've spent the better part of a week discovering everything that wasn't included."

Arch nodded.

Zero: "The hidden residents. The service galleries. The independent shutter. The research equipment. Now this."

She paused.

Zero: "It's like the building has two different histories."

Arch turned toward her.

Arch: "How do you mean?"

Zero considered the question.

Zero: "There's the history the company wrote down. And there's everything that actually happened."

Arch looked back at the map.

Zero continued.

Zero: "And we're trying to govern the second one using records from the first."

For a long moment, Arch said nothing.

Then he nodded slowly.

Arch: "That's a very important distinction."

Zero smiled.

Zero: "I do have my moments."

Arch looked thoughtful.

Arch: "It also means we need to understand how those records were created. Which systems were omitted deliberately. Which were simply forgotten. And which may still be operating according to old authority."

Zero studied him.

Zero: "Aye."

She hesitated.

Zero: "And maybe who those old rules were meant to protect."

Arch looked toward her.

Zero: "Or control."

The room went quiet.

Outside, the last traces of twilight disappeared.

Then Nexus spoke.

Nexus: "Zero. The preserved observations are available for your review."

Zero turned.

Zero: "Thank you."

Nexus: "There is an additional discrepancy."

Zero closed her eyes.

Zero: "Of course there is."

The architectural map shifted.

Level Twelve illuminated.

The instrument platform appeared.

A line traced the documented route of the communications equipment.

Then another line appeared.

Faint.

Incomplete.

Leading away from the platform.

Back into the research floor.

Zero leaned closer.

Zero: "What is that?"

Nexus: "A connection referenced in an archived maintenance diagram. The physical route has not been verified."

Zero studied it.

The line ended near the same service area she'd visited on her first morning.

The warm door.

The unexplained electrical supply.

The place where Glitch had first detected the green signal.

She felt the familiar excitement rise.

Then checked herself.

That area was associated with several unanswered questions.

It did not follow that all of them shared an explanation.

Zero: "Does this prove that the green interface is connected to the transmitter?"

Nexus: "No."

Zero nodded.

Zero: "Good."

She looked at the line again.

Zero: "Does it give us somewhere useful to investigate?"

Nexus: "Yes. Subject to the necessary technical and access assessments."

Zero smiled.

Zero: "Now we're getting somewhere."

She opened her notebook.

The pages were filling rapidly.

Emergency routes.

Unknown systems.

Resident consent.

Administrative boundaries.

The green circle.

The external transmitter.

The possible reply.

For a moment, she felt overwhelmed.

Not frightened, exactly.

Just aware of how much there was to understand.

Then she remembered something Limen had said.

*Those are different questions.*

She looked at the map.

And realized that might be the most useful lesson she'd learned all week.

Not every mystery needed to have the same answer.

Not every strange signal came from the same source.

Not every boundary existed for the same reason.

And not every door was waiting for her to open it.

Zero closed the notebook.

Zero: "All right, then."

Arch looked over.

Arch: "What?"

She smiled.

Zero: "Tomorrow, we find out where that line actually goes."

Arch nodded.

Arch: "With a proper team."

Zero: "Aye."

Arch: "And appropriate authorization."

Zero: "Aye."

Arch: "And no improvising with unknown electrical equipment."

Zero sighed.

Zero: "You take all the romance out of engineering."

He laughed.

Zero looked toward the city.

Then toward the sky.

For the first time, she understood something that hadn't quite occurred to her during the battle for the Nexus.

Defeating Mr. Null had ended one struggle.

It had not ended the systems that made his power possible.

Some of those systems had survived because people needed them.

Some had survived because nobody knew they existed.

And somewhere among them, something had continued communicating long after the man who commanded the building had disappeared.

She didn't yet know what waited at the other end.

But she knew one thing.

The Nexus was no longer merely receiving the consequences of its past.

It was still participating in them.

And beyond the atmosphere, somewhere along an invisible path of radio waves, there might already be someone—or something—listening.

Zero looked at the stars.

Zero: "Well, then."

She folded her arms.

Zero: "Let's hope you're a better conversationalist than the last bastard who owned this place."

The stars offered no reply.

Not one she could hear.

But inside the Nexus, the communications equipment remained active.

And the next transmission window was approaching.

*End of Chapter Seven.*
