## Preservation rule

Keep the original observation, observing speaker/Node, date if known, source, evidence and approval status, and later interpretation distinct. A useful observation need not be an approved principle or implemented feature. Illustrative examples are not transcripts. Never manufacture a source, test, receipt, author, or approval. The Architect retains final editorial and project authority.

The first chapter is the complete existing *Circle of Inquiry* text from the public page, with a separately labeled editorial Node companion. The other twenty chapters are **draft editorial reconstructions** of the twenty flows described in conversation, not verified as a canonical, exhaustively sourced list.

## Editor and Node review path

1. Locate the earliest attributable source for the observation. Preserve its original vocabulary rather than silently harmonizing it to a modern label.
2. Distinguish Architect statement, Node interpretation, approved direction, implemented code, and verified test result.
3. Compare the chapter's worked example and Node instruction against the actual cited source. Mark any reconstruction clearly.
4. Identify superseded assumptions and boundaries before publication. Keep speculative or sensitive details unpublished until separately approved.
5. Request a specific Architect editorial disposition for the chapter (accept, revise, defer, exclude). Editing the register does not grant implementation authority.
6. Preserve provenance and publication revision. Re-run Portal routes, static navigation, keyboard and small-screen checks.
